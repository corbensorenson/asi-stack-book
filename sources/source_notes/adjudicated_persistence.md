## Thesis

The manuscript identifies an **Adaptive Commit Boundary** between transient
experience and durable influence. A system crosses this boundary whenever an
episode changes weights, memory, data, prompts, tools, tests, evaluators,
routing, agent topology, interfaces, organizational routines, or institutions
in a way that can affect future behavior. Such a transition is not passive
storage. It is a consequential effect whose evidence, authority, scope,
qualification, monitoring, descendant reach, and revocation obligations should
be adjudicated explicitly.

The paper calls the governing discipline **Adjudicated Persistence**. It
separates experience records, lesson hypotheses, persistence dispositions,
concrete realizations, qualification leases, and authority grants. It then
treats placement as a portfolio decision across heterogeneous adaptation loci,
not as an automatic choice to fine-tune, remember, or proceduralize.

## Mechanisms

1. **Adaptive Commit Boundary.** Every transition from transient evidence to
   durable causal influence is an admission event with a declared adaptive
   identity and resolution.
2. **Six-object separation.** Experience, lesson, disposition, realization,
   qualification, and authority remain distinct. Support is not
   implementation; implementation is not qualification; qualification is not
   authority.
3. **Learning eligibility.** Integrity, decision-time reconstruction, outcome
   maturity, causal identifiability, evaluator adequacy, protected positives,
   authorized use, and monitorability are checked before a lesson can change an
   actor.
4. **Cross-Surface Adaptation Assignment.** Candidate lessons are assigned to
   portfolios spanning parametric, mnemonic, procedural, semantic,
   orchestration, structural, evaluative, environmental, and institutional
   loci.
5. **Commitment profile.** Scope, binding strength, persistence, coupling,
   authority, irreversibility, and descendant reach form a multidimensional
   commitment burden rather than a binary persistent/not-persistent label.
6. **Evidence-Commitment Matching.** Broader, stronger, more coupled, more
   authoritative, less reversible, and farther-reaching realizations cannot
   require weaker admission evidence merely because they are convenient.
7. **Minimum Sufficient Persistence.** Prefer the least committing admissible
   portfolio that meets efficacy, coverage, capability-preservation,
   observability, and recovery constraints. Minimum commitment is not minimum
   artifact count.
8. **Guarded compilation and deoptimization.** Realizations retain explicit
   `ADMIT`, `DENY`, and `UNKNOWN` routes, material-change invalidation,
   re-expansion triggers, fallback, and decompilation or retirement paths.
9. **Transactional promotion.** Snapshot qualification, compare-and-swap
   promotion, staged commitment, effect-aware rollback or compensation,
   complete denominators, and expiring qualification leases prevent stale or
   partial evidence from silently becoming durable authority.
10. **Counterfactual observability and deliberation reserve.** Compiled fast
    paths must preserve enough slower solving and shadow comparison to reveal
    drift, suppressive errors, lost alternatives, and competence starvation.
11. **Persistence carrying cost and adaptation debt.** Storage, retrieval,
    verification, monitoring, coordination, rigidity, assurance, revocation,
    and descendant-cleanup costs travel with the realization.
12. **Non-self-ratifying meta-compilation.** A compiler may propose changes to
    its own adjudication and compilation machinery, but it cannot be the sole
    source of evidence, evaluation, authority, and promotion for those changes.
13. **LocusBench.** Matched terminal outcomes are generated from different
    causal defects so systems must identify whether the durable response belongs
    in parameters, memory, retrieval, tools, orchestration, world models,
    evaluators, topology, environment, specification, or nowhere yet.

## Evidence Supplied

The paper supplies a formal ontology, typed states, equations, four bounded
propositions, six empirical conjectures, three primary algorithms plus
monitoring and meta-compiler algorithms, a commitment lattice, persistence
cost model, threat taxonomy, reference architecture, packet schema,
lexicographic acceptance order, LocusBench preregistration template, five
figures, and forty-two references. These are specifications and falsification
assets, not observed outcomes.

The bounded propositions concern outcome-only placement non-identifiability,
single-locus insufficiency under mixed requirements, the impossibility of one
timeless open-world performance ratchet under legitimate objective change, and
monotone evidence burden under commitment dominance. Their arguments have not
been independently formalized or checked in this repository.

## Failure Modes

- A fabricated, incomplete, strategically generated, or hindsight-rewritten
  experience is admitted for learning.
- A bad outcome punishes a defensible process, while lucky success compiles a
  defective process.
- A memory, model, tool, evaluator, environment, or specification defect is
  repaired at the wrong locus.
- A low-evidence lesson acquires global scope, strong binding, broad authority,
  irreversible effects, or uncontrolled descendant reach.
- Multiple individually useful repairs conflict when composed.
- A compiled fast path suppresses the slower route needed to discover that it
  is stale or wrong.
- Persistence costs are displaced into retrieval, monitoring, human review,
  revocation, or descendant cleanup and omitted from the comparison.
- A compiler improves the metric that judges the compiler and ratifies itself.
- Revocation updates one store but leaves descendants, fine-tunes, tests,
  tools, publications, or organizational routines active.
- Institutionalization transfers burden or authority without legitimacy,
  affected-party notice, appeal, or exit.
- Detailed forensic retention exceeds privacy and authorized-use boundaries.

## Chapter Decision

Add `adjudicated-persistence-and-the-adaptive-commit-boundary` as a distinct
chapter between Governed Operations and Policy Optimization. The chapter owns
the prior cross-surface commitment decision: whether an experience may become
durable influence, which portfolio of loci should carry it, and what evidence,
authority, qualification, monitoring, deoptimization, and descendant closure
the commitment requires.

This is not a super-owner. Evidence States owns support semantics; Artifact
Graphs owns lineage; memory, data, procedures, policy, organizations, and model
training own their realization mechanisms; Stable Capability Fields and
Readiness own consumer qualification and admission; Governance owns legitimate
authority; Operations owns incident containment and recovery; Resource
Economics owns cost accounting; and Recursive Self-Improvement owns changes to
the improvement machinery. The new chapter owns the transaction that chooses
among and coordinates those loci without inheriting their authority.

## Claims To Add Or Update

- Treat durable adaptation as a consequential effect, not an incidental write.
- Declare the adaptive identity and resolution for every persistence claim.
- Preserve six-object separation across experience, lesson, disposition,
  realization, qualification, and authority.
- Require learning eligibility before selecting an update mechanism.
- Compare portfolios across heterogeneous loci instead of defaulting to model
  weights, memory, data, prompts, or procedures.
- Match evidence and legitimate authority to the full commitment profile.
- Prefer minimum sufficient persistence subject to useful coverage,
  observability, recovery, and protected-capability constraints.
- Preserve `UNKNOWN`, slow paths, shadow comparison, and deoptimization.
- Track material changes, descendants, carrying costs, revocation closure, and
  residuals through the entire lifecycle.
- Forbid sole-source self-ratification by meta-compilers.

## Proof Or Test Candidates

- Mechanize a finite outcome-only placement non-identifiability witness.
- Mechanize finite commitment dominance and monotone evidence obligations.
- Prove that an adaptation transaction cannot activate without distinct lesson,
  realization, qualification, and authority records.
- Prove that a meta-compiler cannot self-promote when proposal, evidence,
  evaluator, and authority independence are all absent.
- Encode finite descendant invalidation and residual closure without claiming
  universal erasure.
- Implement Tier-1 LocusBench matched-collision tasks with exact verifiers,
  injected defect loci, complete candidate portfolios, placement regret,
  commitment overshoot, and equal-total-cost accounting.
- Test deliberation-reserve and deoptimization behavior against a compiled-only
  baseline under induced drift.

## Open Questions

- Which features make a lesson's causal locus identifiable enough for a
  durable commitment rather than evidence retention or investigation?
- How should mixed-locus portfolios be searched without multiplying correlated
  guards, hidden coupling, and maintenance cost?
- Which commitment dimensions admit a defensible partial order across domains?
- How much shadow traffic or deliberation reserve is enough to preserve
  observability without eliminating compilation benefit?
- How should revocation close over model derivatives, organizational routines,
  publications, and external effects that cannot be erased?
- What independence standard is achievable for a meta-compiler that changes
  the evaluators and records used to judge it?
- Which claims in the cited 2025-2026 literature survive full-paper review and
  independent reproduction?
