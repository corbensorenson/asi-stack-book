## Thesis

Long-lived AI requires stable semantic and claim identities that survive changes to models, words, embeddings, indexes, sources, and ontologies. Source state, representation, active context, evidence, belief, and action authority must remain distinct and migrate explicitly when dependencies change.

## Mechanisms

The paper unifies semantic identity, evidence provenance, typed justification artifacts, belief revision, context admission, memory residency, active information acquisition, semantic migration, and adversarial review. Its distinctive contribution is the joined state-separation and migration architecture.

## Failure Modes

- Persistent identifiers preserve names while meaning drifts unnoticed.
- Retrieved or highly ranked content is treated as true, complete, current, or authorized.
- A formal proof launders a mistaken natural-language interpretation or unverified translation.
- Context compaction deletes dissent, contradiction, provenance, or uncertainty needed by a later consumer.
