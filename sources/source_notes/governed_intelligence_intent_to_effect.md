## Thesis

A consequential AI task should be governed as a sequence of typed transformations from principal and accepted intent to an independently observed effect. Requests, plans, capabilities, authority, actions, effects, observations, verification, readiness, and recovery remain separate objects; no successful transition inherits the standing of the next.

## Mechanisms

The paper joins requirements engineering, semantic compilation, planning, delegation, capability security, asynchronous work custody, effect commitment, independent observation, and recovery into one end-to-end lifecycle. Its distinctive contribution is the complete noninheritance path from natural-language objective to reconciled world state.

## Failure Modes

- A request is silently hardened into a task contract or authority grant.
- A plan, route, capability record, or tool acknowledgement stands in for permission or observed state.
- Verification is generalized into readiness, or internal rollback is narrated as recovery from irreversible external consequences.
- The governance path adds ceremony without improving effect control, recovery, or useful completion against a competent simpler baseline.

## Open Questions

- Which lifecycle objects can safely share one implementation while retaining independent state, authority, and failure semantics?

- Under matched resources, when does the complete lifecycle outperform a sandboxed agent with tests, logs, and human review?
