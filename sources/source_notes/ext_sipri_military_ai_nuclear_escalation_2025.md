## Thesis

SIPRI analyzes how military AI outside nuclear weapon systems can still alter
nuclear escalation pathways. Relevant mechanisms include opaque
decision-support recommendations, automation bias, compressed decision time,
false confidence, counterforce capability, threats to second-strike
survivability, and interactions among technical and socio-political factors.

## Failure Modes

- Strategic stability is interaction- and doctrine-dependent; no single
  technical metric determines it.
- A simulation or expert judgment cannot reproduce a real nuclear crisis.
- The source supports risk mechanisms and scenario variables, not operational
  targeting, force posture, or escalation advice.

## Mechanisms

- Trace how AI changes information, confidence, warning, decision speed,
  command relationships, adversary interpretation, entanglement, and escalation.

## Evidence

SIPRI supplies scenario-based peace and security analysis, not a measured net
effect for a particular system or doctrine.

## Claims To Add Or Update

- Treat strategic stability as an interaction claim whose direction can change
  across doctrine, timing, adversary, and proliferation assumptions.
