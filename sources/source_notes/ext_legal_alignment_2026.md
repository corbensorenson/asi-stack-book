## Thesis

The paper develops a research agenda for AI behavior compatible with
legitimate legal rules, legal interpretation methods, and institutions. Legal
alignment is a governed input and process, not a synonym for moral alignment
or an answer that a language model may authoritatively invent.

## Mechanisms

- jurisdiction- and time-specific legal source retrieval;
- authority ranking and interpretation methods;
- conflict, ambiguity, exception, and change handling;
- institutional review and contestation;
- separation of legal, moral, constitutional, and policy authority.

## Evidence

The source is a conceptual research agenda. It does not provide legal advice,
prove model compliance, resolve contested law, or establish the legitimacy of
any particular institution or deployment.

## Failure Modes

- stale or wrong-jurisdiction law;
- model-generated interpretation treated as authoritative;
- unjust law equated with moral alignment;
- conflicts and discretion hidden behind a single label;
- emergency exceptions becoming permanent;
- technical conformance presented as public legitimacy.

## Claims To Add Or Update

- Legal authority packets should bind jurisdiction, time, authoritative text,
  interpretation source, conflicts, rights floor, discretion, review forum,
  exceptions, expiry, and change authority.
- Law, morality, constitutional commitment, organizational policy, and
  technical enforcement remain distinct layers.

## Open Questions

- How should systems route irreducible legal ambiguity?
- Which institutional records are machine-actionable without laundering
  authority?
- How should cross-jurisdiction conflicts affect deployment and refusal?
