## Thesis

A Ratcheting Generative System turns development pressure into durable,
governed structure. A benchmark or live task exposes the current frontier;
attempts produce successes and failures; failures remain in residual escrow;
repeated successful trajectories can become verified parameterized tools;
mastered behavior becomes a regression floor; a seven-level intervention
ladder diagnoses the next change; and a harder frontier supplies new pressure.

The paper's core rule is **improve the frontier without losing the floor**.
Growth is not a score increase alone. It is a transaction that preserves prior
obligations, keeps the unresolved tail live, converts repetition into qualified
procedural memory, separates flexible reasoning from compiled execution and
hard reflexes, and changes architecture only under a residual-specific
hypothesis.

RGS is best understood as a precursor synthesis. It joins ideas later expanded
in Benchmaxxing, Compact Generative Systems, Cognitive Loop Closure, and
Ratcheting Modular Intelligence. Its distinctive retained contribution is the
early complete join plus explicit threshold-decay, Frontier Momentum, residual
recurrence, escrow-budget, three-mode, four-ledger, and seven-phase policies.

Repeated wording and mechanisms across the lineage should be counted once.
RGS can still supply earlier or sharper formulations—especially residual

The optional reference substrate cites liquid networks, KANs, HDC/VSA, active
inference, Rust, WebAssembly, runtime verification, and barrier-like safety
controls as candidate components or analogies. It neither reproduces those
works nor shows that their advertised properties compose.

RGS does not claim that benchmarks perfectly measure intelligence; scores
always transfer; every repetition deserves compilation; tools replace
reasoning; fine-tuning or scale are obsolete; architecture may never change
early; open-world verification is absolute; threshold decay applies to
safety-critical failures; public benchmarks should be ignored; or human
judgment can be removed.

## Formal Object Model

The development-cycle state is
`S_t = (A_t, theta_t, D_t, I_t, M_t, T_t, B_t, R_t, E_t)`: architecture,
parameters, data, inference procedure, memory/state, tool registry, benchmark
frontier, regression suite, and residual escrow/failure map. The ratchet asks
frontier performance to rise by more than `epsilon` while regression
performance falls by no more than `delta`. A graduating benchmark joins the
regression suite, its unsolved cases join escrow, and a new frontier opens.

The equations are architectural sketches. Scalar performance can hide
subgroup, tail, safety, contamination, evaluator, cost, and task-identity
changes. The printed residual set also assumes known labels and a binary error
relation. The canonical book interprets the rule as a claim-scoped vector with
uncertainty, vetoes, complete denominators, exact instrument and system
identity, and explicit non-tradeoff constraints.

For benchmark `b`, RGS proposes an initial mastery threshold, subgroup floor,
critical-failure veto, patience window, decay rate, and absolute floor. After
stalled improvement, the ordinary threshold can decline toward the floor. The
Frontier Momentum predicate advances when current performance clears the
decayed threshold, every required subgroup clears its floor, and critical
failures satisfy a zero or near-zero domain bound. Remaining failures enter
escrow.

The paper's 90% initial and 70% floor examples are not defaults. Threshold
decay must not reward waiting, lower a safety requirement, change the target,
hide uncertainty, or erase a consequential subgroup. A defensible policy needs
prospective task identity, improvement definition, patience, slope, floor,
budget, uncertainty, subgroup and tail rules, critical vetoes, residual
severity, and a reason that moving forward dominates continued work.

## Conceptual Primitives

- **Pressure surface:** benchmark or task family that exposes missing
  capability without becoming a permanent definition of intelligence.
- **Active compression:** turning experience into reusable state, procedure,
  tool, benchmark, residual map, or architectural structure.
- **Compact generative structure:** seed, rules, state, residual, verification,
  and governance/generation interface that carries more behavior than its
  visible description while retaining full burden.
- **Loop closure:** governed conversion of repeated trajectories into a
  parameterized tool with preconditions, effects, verification, monitoring,
  fallback, and retirement.
- **Mastery threshold:** claim-scoped graduation predicate, not a universal
  score target.
- **Subgroup floor:** category-level non-compensation constraint.
- **Critical-failure veto:** failure class that blocks graduation regardless
  of aggregate performance.
- **Frontier Momentum:** advance under threshold, subgroup, and critical-veto
  conditions while escrowing the unresolved tail.
- **Residual escrow:** append-only custody for failures, ambiguity, benchmark
  defects, uncertainty, and hard cases after bounded graduation.
- **Recurrence promotion:** transition from preserved tail to active diagnosis
  when a defensibly shared residual cluster repeats across later frontiers.
- **Public calibration track:** periodic common comparison kept separate from
  the private/live internal optimizer.
- **Interpreter, compiled-tool, and reflex modes:** novel reasoning,
  qualified procedural execution, and deadline-bound containment paths.

## Mechanisms

### Pressure-to-structure loop

The system exposes the frontier, attempts it, analyzes failures, preserves
repeated successes, verifies reusable structure, locks in mastered capability,
advances the frontier, and keeps unresolved failure visible. Success and
failure create different artifacts. Success may create a tool candidate;
failure creates residual custody and diagnosis. Neither automatically changes
support, readiness, routing authority, or architecture.

Active compression includes model parameters, memory, procedures, tools,
benchmarks, and residual maps. Compression is beneficial only if future work
becomes cheaper or more reliable without exporting unpriced verification,
human, safety, fallback, or residual burden. Compactness does not substitute
for useful behavior.

### Mastery decay and Frontier Momentum

Perfect performance is rejected as an automatic ordinary graduation rule
because noisy or low-value tails can trap the curriculum. The opposite error
is graduating by average and forgetting the tail. RGS combines an overall
threshold with subgroup floors, critical vetoes, stalled-effort decay, and
escrow.

The policy remains dangerous if “stalled” is chosen after seeing results, if
the threshold falls through calendar time, if teams underinvest to trigger
decay, or if a changing evaluator makes apparent progress incomparable. The
book therefore treats waiting as no intervention and requires a stable
construct, prospective decay schedule, adequate attempt record, uncertainty,
absolute floors, tail custody, and no ordinary decay for critical obligations.

### Residual escrow lifecycle and budget

Escrow prevents both tail obsession and tail erasure. An item can be reattempted
and, after consistent solution, become a regression item. A recurring cluster
can be promoted to active diagnosis or a future frontier. An instrument audit
can classify an item as noisy, ambiguous, mislabeled, unsolvable, repaired, or
retired. Age, renaming, or a better aggregate is not closure.

The Recurrence Promotion Rule uses repeated appearance above a threshold as a
signal that a tail may be a real wall. Surface similarity is insufficient: the
cluster needs a stable obligation or plausible shared mechanism, evaluator
sensitivity, recurrence window, denominator, and alternative explanations.
Otherwise a classifier can manufacture walls by merging unrelated errors.

RGS offers illustrative allocations across frontier, regression, escrow, and
public calibration. These percentages are not policy defaults. Escrow requires
protected capacity so it is not forgotten, but allocation should respond to
severity, criticality, recurrence, uncertainty, information value, exposure,
review capacity, frontier opportunity cost, and expiry. Critical residuals can
block routes regardless of budget; low-value ordinary residuals can remain
preserved without consuming the entire frontier.

### Public calibration boundary

Public benchmarks provide historical continuity, field comparison, and
credibility. Private, temporal, live, and diagnostic instruments are usually
better internal steering surfaces. The public track answers “how do we compare
under a shared setting?” while the internal frontier asks “what should we
improve next?” Repeated tuning, prompting, submissions, feedback, and public
solution access consume calibration independence and must be recorded.

### Cognitive loop closure

The ten stages are trajectory logging, loop detection, abstraction, active
parameter discovery, synthesis, verification, registration, routing, runtime
monitoring, and revision/retirement. Observed constants are probed through
historical, counterfactual, synthetic, adversarial, environment, or supervisory
tests before becoming invariants. Tool acceptance weighs reuse and expected
savings against creation, maintenance, verification, drift, and risk.

The detailed mechanism is owned by Cognitive Loop Closure. RGS establishes how
tool promotion fits the broader ratchet and why tools remain versioned,
monitored, retireable, and subordinate to risk and execution mode.

### Three execution modes and tiers

Interpreter mode handles novelty, ambiguity, missing parameters, and unverified
families. Compiled-tool mode handles known tasks under valid preconditions,
verification, risk, and authority. Reflex/failsafe mode performs deadline-bound
containment for safety, security, financial, deployment, or physical boundaries.
Fresh deliberation is not a safe fallback when its tail latency exceeds the
remaining margin.

Execution tiers run from text template through structured workflow, typed
function, sandbox, memory-safe runtime, and real-time reflex. Risk tiers run
from low to critical. Least-powerful-sufficient execution is useful, but the
paper does not validate Rust, WebAssembly, a sandbox, memory safety, real-time
behavior, or a safety controller.

### Ledgers and intervention ladder

The benchmark ledger holds instrument role, thresholds, floors, vetoes,
contamination, quality, transfer, cost, escrow, and retirement. The model
ledger holds architecture, data, training, inference, scores, residuals, tool
registry version, regressions, cost, safety, and next wall. The tool registry
holds trajectories, parameters, pre/postconditions, verification, risk,
runtime, usage, failures, version, and retirement. The residual ledger holds
source, type, cluster, severity, schedule, recurrence, disposition, and notes.

The seven interventions are instrument audit, data, training, inference, loop
closure, bridge benchmark, and architecture. Skips need reasons; each attempt
needs matched budgets and a predicted residual-specific signature. A bridge is
valid only when success predicts transfer to the blocked target. Architecture
change asserts that mechanism `X` should improve residual class `B` while
preserving floor `R`, not merely that a larger bundle scored higher.

### High-bandwidth embodied logging

RGS separates raw telemetry, events, semantic state, active skill/controller,
and residuals. Raw detail is retained around anomalies, near misses, reflexes,
failures, and representative successes under a joint memory budget. This idea
is already more fully integrated from RMI and Cognitive Loop Closure in
Embodied Control. RGS is retained as lineage, not added as redundant chapter
support.

## Interfaces and State Transitions

The RGS system joins Benchmark Instrument, Model/System, Tool Card/Registry,
and Residual Escrow records. Readiness consumes those records but remains the
authority for route eligibility. Evidence States remains the authority for
book-claim support. Runtime and Security own execution and effects.

Benchmark states include frontier, diagnostic, graduated, regression, live,
public calibration, contaminated, repaired, retired, and historical. Residual
states include newly failed, escrowed, scheduled for reattempt, consistently
solved, regression, recurring diagnostic, benchmark defect, repaired, and
retired. Tool states include candidate, probed, tested, probationary, active,
monitored, revised, quarantined, and retired. These graphs are non-monotonic;
drift or changed identity can narrow or revoke prior eligibility.

## Evidence, Evaluation, and Falsifiers

The source supplies no execution evidence. Its coding-agent cycle is an
illustration of moving from simple tasks to repository work, compiling a
repository-test procedure, and opening a harder frontier. It does not report a
tool, benchmark score, saved cost, reliable parameter discovery, or transfer.

A serious campaign would compare the joined ratchet with strong expert-led and
simpler benchmark-development baselines. It would preregister thresholds,
decay, floors, vetoes, recurrence, budgets, intervention order, tool criteria,
and architecture hypotheses; use natural and adversarial workloads; preserve
all attempts; and measure useful throughput, regressions, tail outcomes,
calibration, transfer, residual information value, wrong diagnoses, tool
failures, safety, latency, complete cost, and governance work.

Scoped falsifiers include threshold decay that rewards waiting or under-effort;
subgroup floors that miss consequential tails; recurrence promotion that merges
unrelated errors; escrow budgets that either starve the tail or freeze the
frontier; bridge benchmarks that do not transfer; loop closure that increases
maintenance or failure cost; compiled tools that corrupt initially correct
behavior; public calibration that predicts internal progress poorly; wrong
intervention diagnoses; architecture changes whose claimed mechanism is not
causal; reflex paths that miss deadlines; and governance cost exceeding the
decision value of the ratchet.

## Failure Modes and Threats

- Residual graveyards with no owner, trigger, budget, or closure path.
- Patience gaming and threshold decay through waiting or underinvestment.
- Tail erasure through averages, or tail obsession that blocks useful frontier
  work indefinitely.
- Critical failures mislabeled as ordinary residuals.
- Recurrence classifiers that manufacture architecture walls.
- Public benchmark contamination, style overfit, leaderboard gaming, and
  calibration-to-optimizer collapse.
- Premature, brittle, overprivileged, or stale tool compilation.
- Interpreter fallback used where hard latency requires a prequalified reflex.
- Bridge detours that never reconnect to the target.
- Ledgers that record activity without changing routing or decisions.
- Optional substrate components treated as a validated integrated design.
- Architecture novelty that receives more data, compute, tuning, or evaluator
  access than cheaper interventions.
- Capability growth outrunning evidence, governance, interpretability, safety,
  or human review capacity.
