## Thesis

The OECD treats neurotechnology and AI as a convergence domain that can support
health and possible enhancement while intensifying concerns about neural data,
mental integrity, autonomy, responsibility, manipulation, consent, and uneven
access. It points to the OECD neurotechnology recommendation as an upstream
governance baseline.

## Failure Modes

- A policy taxonomy does not establish that a neural interface works or is
  safe.
- “Enhancement” is not one outcome and must be separated from restoration,
  assistance, monitoring, diagnosis, and coercive use.
- Jurisdictional and ethical recommendations do not substitute for participant
  consent, clinical evidence, device security, or longitudinal follow-up.

## Evidence

This is intergovernmental policy synthesis about technology convergence, not a
clinical study or validation of a device or governance regime.

## Claims To Add Or Update

- Govern neuro-AI convergence across institutional boundaries and preserve
  cognitive-rights, access, and uncertainty rather than relying on one sector.
