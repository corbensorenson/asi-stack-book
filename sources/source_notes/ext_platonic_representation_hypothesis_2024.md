## Thesis
Huh, Cheung, Wang and Isola hypothesize that stronger models trained on different data and modalities increasingly share representation geometry.

## Mechanisms
The paper compares kernels or neighborhood relations rather than requiring identical coordinates. Its idealized contrastive argument links representations to event co-occurrence.

## Evidence
Reported vision–language alignment trends motivate the hypothesis. They do not establish universal convergence or a deployed world model.

## Failure Modes
The theoretical argument assumes bijective deterministic observations. Section 6 explicitly discusses lossy/stochastic observations, unequal information and abstract concepts as limitations. Shared geometry does not establish causal correctness, complete grounding, legitimate authority or model interchangeability.

## Claims To Add Or Update
Keep empirical alignment, a sufficient-condition theorem, task transfer and physical prediction separate. This external paper is not Sorenson's `platonic_world_model` source and supplies no validation of that source's semantic constitution.

## Open Questions
How does task-relevant alignment behave when two modalities do not contain the same information?
