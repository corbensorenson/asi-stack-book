## Thesis

The review separates incoherent electro-optical, coherent electro-optical, and
all-optical neural systems and identifies optical/electrical conversion,
nonlinearity, amplification, time-domain processing, precision, and integration
as central design variables. This is the right level for the book: a photonic
kernel has a different state and conversion frontier, not merely a faster GPU.

## Failure Modes

- Projected throughput, efficiency, and density are not deployed end-to-end
  results.
- Data movement, conversion, control, calibration, precision, noise,
  programmability, and training can consume component gains.
- Optical acceleration does not imply reasoning, reliability, or safety gains.

## Book Chapters Supported

- `replaceable-cognitive-substrates-beyond-transformer-monoculture`: optical
  kernel contract.
- `physical-compute-infrastructure-energy-and-environmental-constraints`:
  conversion and system cost.

## Mechanisms

- Map neural operations to integrated photonic computation while retaining
  electronic memory, conversion, control, calibration, and nonlinear stages.

## Evidence

The source is a peer-reviewed review of opportunities and challenges, not a
deployment result or general superiority finding.

## Claims To Add Or Update

- Measure photonic advantage at the useful governed workload boundary, not at
  an isolated optical primitive.

## Open Questions

- When do conversion, memory, precision, packaging, thermal, training, and
  control costs erase or preserve the component gain?
