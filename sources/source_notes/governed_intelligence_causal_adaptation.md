## Thesis

An outcome does not identify a lesson, and a lesson does not identify its correct persistence locus. Adaptive systems need an explicit causal process from evidence through judgment and credit to qualified durable structure, followed by a future-task intervention that establishes whether the structure transfers.

## Mechanisms

The paper combines learning-process topology, persistence admission, commitment profiles, counterfactual diagnosis, competence-dependent learning regimes, procedure induction, and verified forward transfer. Its central scientific object is the causal organization and consequence of adaptation under a sufficiently stable learning process.

- Match persistence commitment to evidence and prefer the minimum sufficient locus or portfolio with explicit retirement.
- Require a frozen future-task intervention before describing stored, invoked, or verified reusable knowledge as beneficial transfer.

## Failure Modes

- Outcome worship or first-story lock-in turns correlation into a durable lesson.
- Every failure becomes a parameter update, or every warning becomes permanent memory.
- Evaluator capture makes the learner improve the judge that approves the learner.
- Storage, invocation, compression, or winner-only traces are reported as forward transfer.

## Open Questions

- Which defects are causally identifiable without unsafe or prohibitively expensive interventions?
- When does cross-locus adjudication beat a competent human or fixed local policy after lifecycle cost?
- Which task lineages support credible cross-family transfer without contaminating the frozen evaluation round?
