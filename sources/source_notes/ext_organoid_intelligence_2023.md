## Thesis

The paper defines organoid intelligence as an emerging biohybrid-computing
program using brain organoids, interfaces, and machine-learning systems. It
also calls for embedded ethics. For the ASI Stack, this is an important
substrate stress test because living tissue changes identity, welfare,
maintenance, contamination, reproducibility, consent, destruction, and
retirement obligations.

## Failure Modes

- Projected efficiency and capability are not demonstrated general-computing
  advantages.
- Biological origin does not establish consciousness or moral patienthood, but
  it increases the need for precaution and provenance.
- A living substrate cannot be treated as a fungible accelerator image.

## Book Chapters Supported

## Mechanisms

The proposed stack couples organoid cultures to stimulation and recording
interfaces, closed-loop training protocols, and machine-learning analysis.
That coupling makes biological provenance, welfare monitoring, contamination
control, and retirement part of the compute contract.

## Evidence

The source establishes a named research program and identifies enabling
laboratory techniques. It is agenda-level evidence for a substrate class, not
performance evidence for general-purpose biocomputing.

## Claims To Add Or Update

- A substrate abstraction is incomplete unless it can express living-material
  provenance, welfare uncertainty, maintenance, and destruction constraints.
- Biohybrid capability claims require matched full-stack energy, reliability,
  reproducibility, and welfare accounting.

## Open Questions

- Which observable conditions should trigger precautionary welfare controls?
- How can experiments distinguish adaptive tissue dynamics from useful,
  reproducible computation?
