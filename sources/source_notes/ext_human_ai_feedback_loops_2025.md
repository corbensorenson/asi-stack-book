## Thesis

Across controlled experiments, repeated interaction with biased AI outputs
changed later human judgments and could create a reinforcing feedback loop.
The important architectural lesson is that the human is not a fixed external
evaluator. Exposure, adaptation, trust, and later behavior can change during
use, so longitudinal human state belongs in the system boundary.

## Failure Modes

- The measured tasks do not establish permanent cognitive change, clinical
  harm, or general social effects.
- Bias transmission is not the only possible adaptation; beneficial learning,
  reactance, correction, and heterogeneous responses also need measurement.
- One output-quality metric cannot stand in for cognition, agency, or welfare.

## Mechanisms

- Repeated AI exposure changes human judgments, which can alter later inputs
  and reinforce coupled bias or other adaptations.

## Evidence

The paper reports controlled human-participant experiments in selected
judgment tasks. It does not establish permanent or population-wide change.

## Claims To Add Or Update

- Treat human state, trust, skill, bias, and dependence as longitudinal system
  variables rather than a fixed external evaluation surface.

## Open Questions

- Which interface, correction, diversity, and withdrawal designs interrupt
  harmful loops without blocking beneficial learning?
