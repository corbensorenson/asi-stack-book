## Thesis

Foundation models can describe many methods civilization developed to improve cognition, but declarative possession does not imply correct recognition, selection, execution, verification, or learning. Human procedures also cannot be copied unchanged into an artificial substrate. CMTQ therefore treats every inherited method as a transport-and-qualification problem: recover its proposed mechanism and conditions, compile a target realization, test it within a bounded scope, and keep its efficacy, execution assurance, and authority independent.

## Distinct contribution to the book

The paper closes an upstream gap between `Cognitive Compilation and Semantic IR` and `Procedural Memory and Cognitive Loop Closure`. Existing chapters compile already accepted obligations or consolidate procedures learned from a stack's own experience. CMTQ owns a different interface: how an external human or institutional method becomes eligible to enter the stack at all.

Its two canonical artifacts are:

- a **Cognitive Technology Record (CTR)**, preserving source practice, proposed mechanisms, assumptions, evidence, contraindications, cost, normative content, provenance, and negative records; and
- a **Qualified Cognitive Protocol (QCP)**, a typed execution contract with applicability predicates, epistemic state, control flow, obligations, forbidden transitions, verifier bindings, failure behavior, budgets, permissions, provenance, and a scope-indexed qualification package.

This boundary warrants a dedicated technical chapter because it owns distinct artifact identities, admission rules, qualification state, and failure modes. The Human Reader should absorb the argument into its existing compilation and procedural-memory units rather than add a twenty-seventh unit.

## Mechanisms mined

1. **Five-gap decomposition.** Separate possession, realization, transport, qualification, and governance. A method can be known but not invoked, invoked but poorly realized, faithfully copied but mechanism-destroying, operational but unqualified for the target, or effective but unauthorized.
2. **Functional transport.** Decompose source method structure into roles, artifacts, operations, constraints, incentives, and feedback; identify mechanism hypotheses; classify substrate dependence and normative content; then copy, transform, hybridize, or reject.
3. **Method ecology.** Store many candidate methods but activate a small, conditionally selected set. Represent dependencies, conflicts, order effects, verifier relationships, cost, risk, and interference.
4. **Typed epistemic transitions.** Preserve distinctions such as observation, report, hypothesis, causal claim, finding, decision, and executed action. Guard transitions that would turn model output into observation, correlation into causation, or candidate action into execution without the required evidence and authority.
5. **Independent axes.** Qualification asks whether the method helps in a declared scope. Assurance asks whether it was actually executed. Authority asks what it may change. No axis implies either of the others.
6. **Three-loop lifecycle.** Inheritance creates evidence-bearing source records and candidate protocols; execution routes and composes qualified protocols; evolution updates beliefs about method performance while keeping candidate changes quarantined until independent promotion.
7. **Two belief states.** Keep beliefs about the world distinct from beliefs about the methods used to know the world.
8. **Negative procedural memory.** Preserve rejected methods and failed mechanism hypotheses so attractive failures are not repeatedly rediscovered.
9. **Root of methodological trust.** Self-revising method science still requires integrity-protected evaluation, independent measurement, held-out environments, external outcomes, legitimate authority, and no-self-certification constraints.
10. **Fixed-model cognitive leverage.** Hold base weights fixed and separately measure method validity, selection validity, execution fidelity, outcome effect, cost, harm, and interactions.

## Failure modes and strongest objections

- Method theater can satisfy artifacts without improving cognition.
- Wrong routing can make a useful method harmful or wasteful.
- Rigid protocols can suppress ambiguity, creativity, or representation change.
- Same-family critics and verifiers can share failure modes.
- Qualification can create method monoculture and evaluator capture.
- Human institutions contain bias, power, and tacit knowledge that do not disappear when compiled.
- Recursive metacognition can create unbounded cost.
- No finite system can revise every methodological root under its own authority without regress or self-ratification.

## Proof and implementation opportunities

- Lean: an unqualified method cannot reach authority-bearing execution; qualification, assurance, and authority projections are non-interchangeable.
- Lean: a candidate protocol may propose but cannot authorize its own promotion or permission expansion.
- Schema/test: validate CTR and QCP identity, scope, obligation, verifier, permission, provenance, expiry, and rollback completeness.
- Project Theseus: implement a bounded CMTQ registry, compile one causal-claim QCP, compare prose possession with compiled execution, and preserve exact receipts and negative controls.
