## Thesis

The paper analyzes autonomous weapon systems through context-specific human
assessment, distinction, proportionality, precautions, legal review, and the
case for international prohibitions and restrictions. It highlights why
preconfigured target categories and operating envelopes do not eliminate the
need to assess the concrete attack context.

## Failure Modes

- The paper explicitly does not resolve every accountability, ethical,
  law-enforcement, proliferation, or human-rights question.
- A technically reliable classifier or controller does not establish lawful
  target selection or proportionality.
- This book must not turn governance discussion into operational weapon or
  targeting guidance.

## Mechanisms

- Bind autonomous-weapon use to context-specific human judgment, legal review,
  operational constraints, predictability, accountability, and affected people.

## Evidence

The paper states the ICRC's legal and policy analysis. It is not an engineering
test or one universally settled legal interpretation.

## Claims To Add Or Update

- Human involvement must be evaluated for the actual use context; interface
  presence alone establishes neither judgment nor legal compliance.

## Open Questions

- How can public assurance remain meaningful when necessary technical evidence
  is restricted and operational detail cannot safely be disclosed?
