## Thesis

Capability improvement is durable only when three ratchets remain linked but distinct: benchmarks preserve changing external pressure, procedures compile repeated successful trajectories into governed tools, and architecture changes only after cheaper interventions fail. “The system improved” is too coarse unless the record says which ratchet moved and what regressions, costs, and authority changes followed.

## Mechanisms

- Benchmark lifecycle: frontier, diagnostic, regression, live, and retired states.
- Residual maps turn failures into structured pressure rather than one leaderboard score.
- An intervention ladder tries benchmark audit, data, training, inference, loop closure, frontier expansion, then architecture change.
- Repeated trajectories become verified tool cards with inputs, preconditions, postconditions, permissions, verification, monitoring, and retirement.
- Tool lifecycle: candidate, proposed, probed, tested, probationary, active, monitored, revised, retired.
- Runtime modes separate interpretation, compiled tools, and reflex/failsafe paths.
- Anti-Goodhart controls include rotation, private holdouts, mutation, multiple metrics, transfer, and capability narratives.
- Tool compilation is justified only when recurrence, quality, cost savings, and automation fit outweigh creation, maintenance, verification, drift, and risk costs.

## Evidence

- No benchmark campaign, tool compiler, architecture-selection study, or measured capability ratchet is reproduced here.

## Failure Modes

- Treating benchmark score movement as capability improvement without transfer or residual analysis.
- Compiling a recurring workflow whose errors or maintenance cost dominate its savings.
- Jumping to architecture change before testing data, training, inference, or procedural interventions.
- Allowing a compiled tool or reflex path to inherit more authority than its interpreted predecessor.
- Counting same-author synthesis as independent corroboration.

## Claims To Add Or Update

- Keep benchmark, procedural, and architectural ratchets as separately observable state transitions.
- Require architecture-change proposals to carry the exhausted lower-cost interventions and their evidence.
- Bind a compiled procedure to lifecycle, monitoring, regression, authority, and retirement rather than treating reuse as automatic improvement.

## Open Questions

- How should value be attributed when one intervention moves several ratchets at once?
- What evidence is sufficient to say an intervention tier is exhausted?
- Can private and mutated benchmarks remain informative under repeated self-improvement?
