## Thesis

Epistemic fit, task fidelity, rights and legitimacy, and authority over real effects are noncompensating achievements. Constitutional governance should preserve affected-party standing, meaningful human control, contestability, operational rights, material exit, and moral-status uncertainty rather than collapsing them into one alignment score.

## Mechanisms

The paper develops four constitutional decision planes, operational rights interfaces, material-exit and fork governance, prospective review of architecture-induced moral uncertainty, and bounded constitutional amendment and emergency authority.

## Failure Modes

- Truth, task success, approval, rights, and effect safety collapse into one alignment score.
- Constitutional prompts, rights, meaningful control, appeal, or exit exist only as labels without reachable consequences.
- Emergency authority becomes a permanent expansion or bypasses later review.
- Moral-status signals are either ignored or allowed to unilaterally widen system authority.
