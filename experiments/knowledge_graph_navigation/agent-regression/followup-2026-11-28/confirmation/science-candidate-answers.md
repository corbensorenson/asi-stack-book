## CS1

Both policies use the same two predictions on each of the 299 reserved SciFact claims, including holds, for **598 model readings per policy**. Each pass pairs the claim with a premise. Pass A sees the title, selected citation sentences, and their immediate neighbors. Pass B sees only the exact sentences proposed for citation. The same frozen MiniLM NLI model performs both readings, so requiring agreement adds no independent-model evidence. On claim 51, pooling released `CONTRADICT` with a citation containing an annotated rationale, and agreement held the claim. ## CS2

Agreement minus pooling was **+1.67 triage utility per 100 claims**, with a **95% paired paper-group bootstrap interval of −2.36 to +5.77**. The frozen rule required a lower bound above zero and a held-out review fraction at or below 50% to switch to mandatory agreement. Agreement failed both conditions, holding 247 of 299 claims (82.6%). Keep pooling as the experimental reference. Pooling also exceeded the review budget, holding 212 of 299 claims. These results apply to the reserved, paper-disjoint local SciFact evaluation with one lexical retriever and one frozen NLI model. ## CS3

On 213 paired SciFact-Open claims, choosing among three papers reduced holds from **140 to 108** (32 fewer) and increased actual input tokens from **61,267 to 185,582** (124,315 more, about 3.03 times as many). Model readings rose from 426 to 1,278. To estimate deployment cost, measure end-to-end wall time, energy or billed compute, and human work such as review time under the intended deployment conditions, then apply the relevant rates. The study measured model input under a two-sentence citation limit. Paper exposure could overlap earlier studies.
