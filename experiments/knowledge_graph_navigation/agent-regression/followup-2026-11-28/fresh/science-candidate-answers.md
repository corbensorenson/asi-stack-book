## FS2

The alternate sentence selector retained annotated sentence **6** and replaced annotated sentence **1** with **2** (zero-based abstract indices). With only sentence selection changed, the run kept the annotated Lukens et al. paper **5811042**, returned **[2, 6]**, and released **CONTRADICT**, scored **unsupported** because the required highlights were incomplete. With only document ranking changed, the run selected unjudged paper **34256713**, returned **[1, 2]**, and **held** its **NEUTRAL** prediction. The TF–IDF baseline returned **[1, 6]** in paper **5811042** and earned a **justified CONTRADICT**. The paired case shows how each change lost justification here. It leaves open how often either failure occurs, which stage matters more across other claims, and whether an unjudged paper could provide valid evidence. ## FS3

Across **213 paired SciFact-Open claims**, choosing among three papers increased unsupported releases from **52 to 81** (**29 additional**) and model readings from **426 to 1,278** (**852 additional**, three times as many). Justified releases rose from **21 to 24**. The study kept the first-paper policy as the reference. Elapsed time, energy, and human review work remain unmeasured. ## FS4

Across **65 paired claims**, TF–IDF at both stages delivered **7 complete highlights, 4 justified answers, and 19,669 input tokens**. Changing both stages delivered **7 complete highlights, 2 justified answers, and 20,737 input tokens**, an increase of **1,068 tokens**. A complete highlight count is an aggregate. The two arms can reach that count on different claims, and justification also requires the released verdict to match the annotation. Equal highlight totals therefore allow different release decisions and justification totals. The run held model calls and ceilings equal and used a finite catalog with incomplete annotations.
