# Protocol for the first larger comparison

Freeze the six panels, 24 questions, rubric, reader, and artifact identities before dispatch. The questions are new to the preceding agent-publication trials. Existing chapters, studies and paper comparisons supply the evidence. The held-out unit is a question; no claim is made that the underlying material was absent from model training. Four questions about source-reported generation and compression sit outside compact evidence discovery. The suite also covers measured runs, paired cases and exact declarations.

Run twelve independent fresh sessions, two per panel across the two editions. Each pair gets the same four questions in the same order. Both start at index.html. Inherit the same model configuration and reasoning effort for each session. Give the same tool and budget to both, and allow one JSON open to batch several clues. Agents may follow discovered book routes or pinned source links in the ASI Stack repository. They may not read other files, use another evidence tool, or see another session or the rubric. Ask for the decision, exact citation and scope, or an explicit statement of what the evidence leaves open. A broken open counts toward the allowance. Limits are 18 opens and 52,000 displayed bytes per session, with up to 5,500 per open.

After all sessions, score the three criteria manually, recording partial-credit reasons. A reviewer aware of the comparison grades each session. Use the published archives and recorded route, focus and response hashes to replay log integrity and costs. Compute panel-paired totals and descriptive percentile intervals over resampled panels. A cost pass requires strictly fewer deduplicated bytes. Quality and scope require scores at least as high as the baseline. The candidate may add no unsupported numerical assertions. State all four gate outcomes. The same questions and reachable materials are paired to control the workload. The six panel pairs do not estimate population variation in agents or tasks.
