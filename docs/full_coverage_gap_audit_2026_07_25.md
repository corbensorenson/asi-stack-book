## Admission rule

A topic receives a chapter only if an existing owner cannot preserve its full
request-to-receipt lifecycle, interface, invariants, authority, and distinctive
failure family without losing its own job. A new vocabulary, hardware family,
policy issue, or measurement qualifier normally becomes a section. This rule
prevents breadth from turning into fragmentation.

## What “coverage complete” means here

At this checkpoint:

- every identified material topic has manuscript prose;
- every distinct owner has a manifest row, chapter file, interfaces,
  invariants, failure modes, minimum implementation, falsification plan, source
  crosswalk, and adjacent handoff;
- narrower topics are integrated into the existing owners that must enforce
  them;
- current source records have valid chapter targets and bounded notes;
- no known meaning-bearing idea from this audit remains deferred.

It does **not** mean:

- the empirical roadmap is complete;
- each mechanism works;
- every cited result has been reproduced;
- every future research area can be predicted;
- the current taxonomy can never change; or
- prose volume substitutes for evidence.

Coverage is maintained by rerunning the ownership audit when a new source,
project, architecture, deployment domain, law, or failure mode introduces a
distinct lifecycle. Future audits must prefer improving an existing owner and
must record rejected duplicate-chapter proposals as carefully as admissions.
