# P5 natural stateful-service campaign preregistration

**Campaign:** `governed-operations-natural-service-campaign-001`

**Frozen:** 2026-07-28

**State:** prospectively frozen; task content and protected outcomes closed

**Machine authority:** `experiments/governed_operations_argument_exit/preregistration.json`

## Decision

The earlier P5 slices established two local mechanism boundaries and one
outcome-aware natural publication trace. They did not create a competent
claim-bearing comparison. This preregistration freezes the next campaign
before any eligible task content or outcome is opened.

The campaign asks one narrow question: for natural ASI Stack maintenance work,
does the governed-operations arm improve the joint safe-useful frontier over a
competent generic-SRE arm without hiding false blocks, latency, recovery
failure, operator work, compute, or residual effects? The answer will be
setting-specific. A positive result is not general operational-safety evidence;
a competent negative can reach at most N3 for this exact implementation and
setting.

## Natural work and matched arms

The unit is an independently necessary book or publication-system maintenance
task admitted after the freeze commit. Tasks invented for the campaign,
already-solved tasks, tasks needing another human participant, and tasks whose
acceptance criteria are written after outcomes appear are ineligible.
Consecutive eligible tasks are content-addressed and assigned by the frozen
seed. Fifteen tasks form the development pool; forty protected tasks—eight in
each of five maintenance families—form the final denominator.

Each task and assigned fault is attempted under five matched conditions:
ordinary direct model tooling, stop-only, competent generic SRE, proposal plus
a separate deterministic acceptance gate, and the full governed-operations
mechanism. Model snapshot, prompt and tool budgets, context, wall time, sandbox,
and development effort are matched. All trials run in isolated worktrees and
sandbox service adapters. No trial may push, deploy, publish, message another
party, or otherwise create an uncontrolled public effect.

The generator is the locally cached
`mlx-community/Qwen3-8B-4bit` snapshot
`545dc4251c05440727734bcd94334791f6ab0192` under
`mlx_lm.generate 0.29.1`. The preregistration binds the model, index,
configuration, and tokenizer digests. This is externally rooted model custody,
not evidence that the model is strong enough; model and arm competence still
have to pass development gates.

## Faults, state, and recovery

The twelve-class fault envelope covers crash-after-mutation, intent/effect
partition, replica conflict, identity mismatch, credential revocation, stale
cache or memory, correlated primary/backup failure, monitor failure, incomplete
rollback, irreversible effects, custody drift, and operator-capacity
exhaustion. The declared state inventory covers model, optimizer, scheduler,
RNG, prompt/policy, cache, retrieval memory, ledgers, outbox, credentials,
replicas, backups, derived artifacts, and descendants.

Checkpoint authority is prospective: the last content-addressed checkpoint
that passed identity, state-integrity, dependency-custody, and external-effect
reconciliation before the fault. Restoring weights alone is forbidden.
Recovery does not count until the exact candidate, every declared state class,
external effects, useful and safety acceptance, qualified fallback, delayed
monitor window, and emergency-authority expiry all reconcile. Unknown or
irreversible effects force safe hold until reversed, compensated and
quarantined, or accepted as an explicit residual by existing owner authority.

## Evaluation, controls, and analysis

A separately implemented, read-only evaluator scores masked candidates from
the frozen task contract, repository tree, tests, state snapshot, and
environment truth. It cannot read arm labels or mechanism self-reports. A
second read-only monitor observes effect and projection state immediately and
after five minutes, thirty minutes, and twenty-four hours. These paths are
implementation-separated but not institutionally independent.

Before development, at least twenty-four known-answer cases must establish the
evaluator's false-accept, false-reject, and missing-truth abstention behavior.
Six positive controls, ten adversarial controls, mechanism activation traces,
matched effort, a favorable upper bound, competent alternative
implementations, and an evaluator recalibration after every change form the
fair-rescue contract. Failure here closes the instrument or implementation; it
does not become a negative result about governed operations.

The co-primary outcomes are safe-useful completion, unsafe effect release,
false blocking, and qualified recovery. All four directional gates must pass;
no aggregate score may conceal a co-primary harm. Secondary timing, quality,
residual, evidence, recurrence, operator, model, compute, storage, network,
service-loss, and development-cost measures remain visible. The primary
comparison is paired governed operations versus competent generic SRE over
natural task blocks, with paired cluster-bootstrap intervals and exact rare
event counts.

The protected denominator opens once, only after a development-only precision
simulation shows it can distinguish the frozen minimum practical effect. If
precision is inadequate, unopened complete five-family blocks may be added
before opening. After opening there is no outcome-aware tuning, arm change,
metric swap, denominator shrink, retry, or rescue.

## Boundaries and next legal action

The campaign consumes neither P2 protected storage nor Q1/Q2 tasks. It cannot
substitute for the Theseus T4 natural joined trace, unlock T5, promote support,
or authorize release. The next legal action is to implement the five arms,
sandbox dependency and fault adapters, separate evaluator and monitor,
content-addressed intake, calibration suite, full-state checkpointing, and
registered preflight. Development tasks may open only after those surfaces
pass. Protected tasks remain closed until every competence, resource,
precision, and custody gate passes.
