## Purpose and reader promise

This outline is the writing source of truth for the independent human-reader
edition of *The ASI Stack*. It is not a filtered copy of the 87-chapter
architecture reference. It composes those technical owners into 26 chapters
that make one cumulative argument: advanced intelligence should be built as a
governed system of separable responsibilities; those responsibilities can be
implemented as a cognitive and operational stack; and that stack can learn,
improve, and scale only when evidence, authority, resources, and recovery stay
explicit.

The intended reader is technically curious but does not need repository
administration, repeated status ledgers, theorem inventories, fixture counts,
or chapter-local AI scaffolding in the main prose. Those details remain
available through compact generated evidence panels and links to the living
architecture reference.

The edition targets 131,000-180,000 words, the exact aggregate of the unit
ranges below. Each unit should read as a finished
book chapter with a concrete opening, one dominant question, a visible causal
argument, honest objections, and a transition that makes the next chapter
necessary. The continuing example is one consequential repository change that
progressively acquires intent, authority, context, plans, evidence, effects,
learning, replacement, and recursive-improvement obligations.

## Composition rules

1. Canonical owner routing is editorial composition, not claim absorption. A
   lead chapter does not inherit the support, proof scope, evidence, or
   authority of any routed owner.
2. Every one of the 87 current technical owners appears exactly once in the
   primary route below. Cross-references may recur, but ownership does not.
3. Each unit must preserve the routed owners' distinct objects, strongest
   objections, failure modes, evidence exits, and non-claims while removing
   repeated lifecycle boilerplate.
4. The human chapter opens with a case or decision, not a status report. Exact
   source, theorem, fixture, and validator counts belong in generated panels.
5. The running example accumulates state. Later units must not silently reset
   its authority, evidence, costs, failures, or unresolved residuals.
6. A chapter closes by stating what has been established, what remains
   uncertain, and why the next chapter is required.
7. Full EPUB, PDF, DOCX, and audio production remains deferred until the
   independent manuscript reaches a major-version content freeze.

# Part I - Why Intelligence Needs a Governed Stack

Part I moves from the book's systems thesis to the social and constitutional
conditions under which a powerful system may act. It should leave the reader
with a precise reason that capability, authority, evidence, and legitimacy
cannot be collapsed into model behavior.

## Unit 1 - ASI Is a Stack, Not a Model

**Lead owner.** `asi-is-a-stack-not-a-model`

**Canonical owner routes.** `asi-is-a-stack-not-a-model`

**Narrative job.** Replace the image of a single superintelligent model with a
system of distinct responsibilities and governed transitions, then establish
noninheritance as the book's central engineering law.

**Central question.** What must remain separate for advanced intelligence to
be understandable, governable, repairable, and replaceable?

**Argument moves.** Begin with a repository change that succeeds but cannot be
reconstructed; distinguish capability, authority, context, planning, effect,
observation, evidence, memory, and improvement; show how typed interfaces make
failure ownership visible; state the monolith objection and the empirical
conditions under which the stack should be simplified.

**Running example.** Decompose one requested repository change into a proposed
goal, bounded authority, context package, plan, effect path, observation path,
evidence obligation, and recovery route.

**Strongest objection.** Explicit layers may discard useful information and
create ceremony that a capable end-to-end model could avoid.

**Evidence that would change the conclusion.** Matched natural deployments in
which a simpler monolith is equally controllable, observable, recoverable,
useful, and affordable would narrow or reject parts of the stack thesis.

**Handoff.** Separation has a cost, so Unit 2 asks whether routing, reuse, and
specialization can pay for the architecture rather than merely decorate it.

**Target length.** 4,500-6,000 words.

## Unit 2 - The Efficient ASI Hypothesis

**Lead owner.** `the-efficient-asi-hypothesis`

**Canonical owner routes.** `the-efficient-asi-hypothesis`

**Narrative job.** State the book's efficiency hypothesis without treating
token reduction, benchmark speed, or smaller models as automatic system-level
improvement.

**Central question.** Can a governed stack produce more accepted useful work
per unit of total lifecycle cost than frontier-model-only operation?

**Argument moves.** Define accepted useful output; enumerate inference,
verification, repair, latency, memory, energy, reviewer, residual, and recovery
costs; introduce routing, caching, specialist computation, and selective
deliberation as hypotheses; require matched comparisons and explicit defeat
conditions.

**Running example.** Compare a frontier-model-only repository change with a
routed path that reuses context, delegates verification, and accounts for
every repair and review step.

**Strongest objection.** Governance and routing overhead may exceed any savings
from specialization or reuse.

**Evidence that would change the conclusion.** Full-cost matched workloads
showing no risk-adjusted quality, throughput, or recovery advantage would
reject or sharply narrow the hypothesis.

**Handoff.** Efficiency is meaningful only inside an authority boundary, which
Unit 3 develops through concrete failure and misuse cases.

**Target length.** 4,500-6,000 words.

## Unit 3 - Authority, Failure, and Misuse

**Lead owner.** `system-boundaries-and-authority`

**Canonical owner routes.** `system-boundaries-and-authority`,
`failure-modes-of-ungoverned-intelligence`,
`dangerous-capability-domains-and-misuse-uplift`,
`military-ai-autonomous-weapons-and-strategic-stability`

**Narrative job.** Show why proposing, approving, executing, observing, and
releasing are different acts, then pressure-test that distinction against
ordinary failure, dual-use uplift, and high-consequence domains.

**Central question.** Who may cause which effect, under what conditions, and
who owns the result when capability outruns authority or observation?

**Argument moves.** Build the proposal-to-effect boundary; introduce scoped
grants, expiry, revocation, and residual ownership; organize failure by broken
responsibility rather than dramatic scenario; examine misuse uplift and
strategic escalation without implying that a schema resolves law or policy.

**Running example.** Let a planner draft a patch while only a current scoped
grant permits a bounded adapter to modify the tree; then test stale credential,
confused-deputy, and dangerous-target variants.

**Strongest objection.** Typed authority can become ceremonial metadata when
the runtime, operator, or institution can bypass it.

**Evidence that would change the conclusion.** Simpler permission systems that
resist revocation, bypass, confused-deputy, escalation, and residual-loss
failures equally well would justify a smaller boundary model.

**Handoff.** Authority cannot survive an untrusted substrate, so Unit 4 moves
downward into security, privacy, computation, and artifact custody.

**Target length.** 5,000-7,000 words.

## Unit 4 - Security, Privacy, and AI Artifact Custody

**Lead owner.** `security-kernel-and-digital-scifs`

**Canonical owner routes.** `security-kernel-and-digital-scifs`,
`adversarial-machine-learning-and-model-attack-surface`,
`privacy-data-rights-and-information-flow-governance`,
`confidential-and-verifiable-ai-computation`,
`model-weight-custody-and-hardware-roots-of-trust`,
`open-weight-release-and-post-release-control`,
`ai-supply-chain-integrity-and-lifecycle-provenance`

**Narrative job.** Establish the trusted base beneath cognitive governance and
follow data, models, dependencies, keys, attestations, and release decisions
through their distinct custody lifecycles.

**Central question.** What must remain outside the model so that untrusted
content, compromised components, private data, and irreversible releases do
not silently acquire authority?

**Argument moves.** Introduce the narrow security kernel and reference
monitor; map training- and inference-time attacks; separate privacy rights
from protected execution; trace weights and dependencies from provenance to
retirement; treat open release as an authority-loss transition rather than a
reversible deployment toggle.

**Running example.** Bind the repository change to exact source provenance,
least-privilege tools, protected secrets, dependency identities, model version,
and a release disposition that cannot be inferred from local success.

**Strongest objection.** Hardware roots, attestations, provenance records, and
policy kernels can all be compromised or produce false assurance about the
larger system.

**Evidence that would change the conclusion.** Evidence that a materially
smaller trusted base preserves confidentiality, integrity, revocation,
provenance, and post-release risk at lower burden would narrow the design.

**Handoff.** Secure records still do not tell us what to believe, so Unit 5
turns from custody to evidence and scalable review.

**Target length.** 5,500-7,500 words.

## Unit 5 - Evidence States and Scalable Oversight

**Lead owner.** `evidence-states-and-claim-discipline`

**Canonical owner routes.** `evidence-states-and-claim-discipline`,
`scalable-oversight-and-adversarial-ai-control`,
`white-box-evidence-interpretability-and-activation-governance`

**Narrative job.** Explain how support changes without laundering nearby
claims, then connect scalable oversight and internal-model evidence to the
same identity-bound discipline.

**Central question.** How can limited evaluators learn from behavior, internal
signals, proofs, and delegated review without letting any one artifact stand
in for truth or safety?

**Argument moves.** Separate claims from receipts and runtime reality; define
support transitions, maximum inference, and defeaters; show where oversight
scales and where it degrades; position white-box methods as bounded evidence
with construct and intervention obligations; preserve disagreement and no-
promotion outcomes.

**Running example.** Let a passing test strengthen one narrow patch claim while
an internal activation probe and delegated reviewer add bounded, noninheriting
evidence rather than promoting the architecture.

**Strongest objection.** Evidence labels and oversight hierarchies may create
an aura of rigor while dependence, weak constructs, and reviewer incapacity
remain unchanged.

**Evidence that would change the conclusion.** Natural correction streams in
which simpler review records produce equal calibration, challenge, and error
recovery would justify a smaller transition system.

**Handoff.** Oversight itself can be manipulated or misunderstand intent, so
Unit 6 centers the human as principal, reviewer, and epistemic target.

**Target length.** 5,000-7,000 words.

## Unit 6 - Human Intent, Control, and Epistemic Security

**Lead owner.** `intent-to-execution-contracts`

**Canonical owner routes.** `human-intent-as-a-formal-input`,
`human-factors-and-meaningful-control-in-oversight`,
`human-ai-communication-persuasion-and-epistemic-security`,
`intent-to-execution-contracts`

**Narrative job.** Turn human intent from an assumed prompt property into a
revisable formal input while accounting for attention, comprehension,
persuasion, disagreement, revocation, and the cost of meaningful control.

**Central question.** How does an ambiguous human request become bounded work
without inventing permission or manipulating the person who must approve it?

**Argument moves.** Preserve ambiguity instead of silently resolving it;
compile purpose, scope, success, authority, evidence duties, and stop rules;
model operator capacity and intervention windows; treat communication and
persuasion as attack surfaces; require correction, appeal, and re-contracting.

**Running example.** Convert a request to improve a chapter into explicit
targets and stop conditions, then confront a persuasive proposal that is
technically coherent but outside the user's intended scope.

**Strongest objection.** Formal intent contracts can harden a mistaken
interpretation and shift genuine human judgment into unread fields.

**Evidence that would change the conclusion.** User studies showing lighter
clarification and control mechanisms produce fewer consequential intent and
manipulation failures at lower burden would narrow the contract.

**Handoff.** Individual intent cannot settle conflicting values, so Unit 7
moves from commands to constitutions and objective formation.

**Target length.** 5,000-7,000 words.

## Unit 7 - Constitutions, Moral Uncertainty, and Objective Formation

**Lead owner.** `constitutional-alignment-substrate`

**Canonical owner routes.** `constitutional-alignment-substrate`,
`inner-alignment-mesa-optimization-and-learned-objective-integrity`,
`moral-uncertainty-and-value-conflict`,
`governed-objective-formation-value-learning-and-goal-integrity`

**Narrative job.** Show how protected commitments, unresolved value conflict,
learned objectives, amendment, and appeal can coexist without allowing an
optimizer to ratify its own purpose.

**Central question.** How should a system act when objectives are learned,
values conflict, and no single preference can legitimately settle the case?

**Argument moves.** Separate constitutional constraints from ordinary goals;
preserve plural value profiles and uncertainty; distinguish observed behavior
from learned objective integrity; define proposal, evaluation, amendment,
appeal, and ratification as different authorities; keep disagreement visible.

**Running example.** A requested repository optimization conflicts with a
protected policy and an affected maintainer's concern, requiring contestable
interpretation rather than silent utility maximization.

**Strongest objection.** A formal constitution may freeze contested values,
centralize power, and provide only the appearance of alignment.

**Evidence that would change the conclusion.** Pluralistic mechanisms with
fewer capture, rigidity, evasion, and review-burden failures would replace or
sharply narrow the proposed substrate.

**Handoff.** Constitutions need legitimate institutions, which Unit 8 treats as
an engineering dependency rather than an assumed source of authority.

**Target length.** 5,000-7,000 words.

## Unit 8 - Institutions, Coordination, and Societal Resilience

**Lead owner.** `institutions-international-coordination-and-public-legitimacy`

**Canonical owner routes.**
`institutions-international-coordination-and-public-legitimacy`,
`societal-resilience-and-misuse-defense`

**Narrative job.** Extend governance from one system to affected publics,
jurisdictions, competing organizations, international coordination, remedy,
and recovery under diffuse or cumulative harm.

**Central question.** What makes an AI governance decision legitimate and
resilient when authority, harms, and affected parties cross institutional
boundaries?

**Argument moves.** Distinguish capability from mandate; trace standing,
participation, jurisdiction, remedy, and appeal; describe resistance,
absorption, recovery, and adaptation as separate capacities; examine
coordination failure, concentration, and gradual loss of human influence.

**Running example.** Move the repository change into a shared infrastructure
dependency where multiple maintainers, users, and jurisdictions have different
authority and exposure.

**Strongest objection.** Institutional machinery can entrench incumbents,
diffuse accountability, and move more slowly than the harms it governs.

**Evidence that would change the conclusion.** Leaner institutional designs
that preserve standing, remedy, coordination, and recovery with less capture
and delay would justify consolidation.

**Handoff.** Part II begins by asking how stable technical capabilities can
change behind these governance boundaries without inheriting readiness.

**Target length.** 4,500-6,500 words.

# Part II - The Cognitive and Operational Stack

Part II follows the repository change through capability replacement,
perception, models, planning, compilation, context, evidence, work, effects,
memory, and inter-stack exchange. It should feel like one operating system
being assembled, not a catalog of unrelated components.

## Unit 9 - Stable Capability Fields and Governed Replacement

**Lead owner.** `stable-capability-fields`

**Canonical owner routes.** `stable-capability-fields`,
`capability-replacement-and-rollback`

**Narrative job.** Separate stable capability identity from changing
implementation identity and make qualification, migration, rollback, and
retirement properties of each replacement rather than inherited reputation.

**Central question.** How can a system change its models, tools, memories, and
substrates without losing the contract that makes those changes governable?

**Argument moves.** Define stable fields and versioned implementations;
separate compatibility from qualification; require matched regression,
migration, rollback, residual, and retirement records; show why predecessor
readiness cannot transfer automatically.

**Running example.** Replace the patch verifier while preserving its exact
contract, attack envelope, evidence duties, authority ceiling, and recovery
route.

**Strongest objection.** Stable field names can hide semantic drift and make
incomparable components appear interchangeable.

**Evidence that would change the conclusion.** Natural replacement studies
showing no continuity, challenge, or recovery benefit beyond conventional
versioning would weaken the abstraction.

**Handoff.** A replaceable capability must still know what happened, so Unit 10
starts the cognitive path with observation trust.

**Target length.** 4,500-6,000 words.

## Unit 10 - Perception and Observation Trust

**Lead owner.** `perception-sensor-fusion-and-observation-trust`

**Canonical owner routes.** `perception-sensor-fusion-and-observation-trust`,
`embodied-agency-real-time-control-and-physical-safety`

**Narrative job.** Establish observations as provenance-bearing, fallible
inputs rather than direct reality, then extend that discipline to real-time
physical control where latency and irreversible effects matter.

**Central question.** When may a system treat sensor, tool, human, or
environmental reports as independent-enough evidence about actual state?

**Argument moves.** Separate observation identity from truth; model common
causes, disagreement, freshness, calibration, and taint; fuse sources without
double counting; connect observation quality to safe control envelopes,
deadlines, fallback, and recovery without claiming physical safety from a
record alone.

**Running example.** Compare file-system, test-runner, reviewer, and deployment
observations, including two reports that agree only because they share the same
stale cache.

**Strongest objection.** Provenance fields cannot guarantee independence,
calibration, or physical truth and may merely document correlated error.

**Evidence that would change the conclusion.** Simpler fusion and control
systems with equal calibration, contradiction detection, latency, and recovery
would justify a thinner trust model.

**Handoff.** Observations become useful when organized into predictions and
causal alternatives, which Unit 11 assigns to governed world models.

**Target length.** 4,500-6,500 words.

## Unit 11 - Governed World Models and Reality Grounding

**Lead owner.** `governed-world-models-and-reality-grounding`

**Canonical owner routes.** `governed-world-models-and-reality-grounding`

**Narrative job.** Keep simulations, predictions, interventions, observations,
and reconciled beliefs distinct while showing how their mismatch drives useful
learning and control.

**Central question.** How can a system reason over possible worlds without
confusing a successful simulation or plausible story with actual state?

**Argument moves.** Represent alternative states and causal assumptions;
freeze predictions before intervention; bind observed outcomes to provenance;
reconcile rather than overwrite disagreement; route calibration failure and
unknown external effects to safe holds.

**Running example.** Predict the patch's effects in a branch, execute a bounded
trial, and reconcile the predicted dependency graph with the observed tree and
test results.

**Strongest objection.** World-model governance may add records without
improving prediction, causal identification, calibration, or action.

**Evidence that would change the conclusion.** Strong predictive and causal
baselines with simpler branch discipline and equal intervention value would
justify collapsing the extra machinery.

**Handoff.** A world model can compare futures, but Unit 12 must decide how a
plan coordinates them without becoming permission.

**Target length.** 4,500-6,000 words.

## Unit 12 - Planning as a Control Layer

**Lead owner.** `planning-as-a-control-layer`

**Canonical owner routes.** `planning-as-a-control-layer`

**Narrative job.** Treat plans as versioned, challengeable control artifacts
that coordinate dependencies and alternatives without granting execution
authority.

**Central question.** How can a plan remain useful when assumptions, context,
costs, and authority change during execution?

**Argument moves.** Define goals, dependencies, assumptions, alternatives, and
verification points; separate reachability from permission; add replanning,
abandonment, and escalation triggers; compare reactive and deliberative control
under uncertainty and time pressure.

**Running example.** Represent inspection, drafting, verification, bounded
application, observation, and rollback as branches whose preconditions can
expire independently.

**Strongest objection.** Detailed plans can be expensive and brittle in
environments that change faster than they can be checked.

**Evidence that would change the conclusion.** Dynamic-task studies in which
direct reactive control is safer, cheaper, and more recoverable would narrow
when explicit planning should activate.

**Handoff.** Plans need executable representations, so Unit 13 follows meaning
through cognitive compilation and search.

**Target length.** 4,500-6,000 words.

# Part III - Learning, Evidence, Efficiency, and Recursive Improvement

Part III follows capability selection, training, persistence, evaluation,
resource use, physical infrastructure, recursive improvement, and integrated
implementation. It closes by returning every proposal to evidence and
authority rather than ending with an unconstrained intelligence explosion.

## Unit 19 - Routing and Replaceable Cognitive Substrates

**Lead owner.** `replaceable-cognitive-substrates-beyond-transformer-monoculture`

**Canonical owner routes.** `routing-heads-and-specialist-cores`,
`replaceable-cognitive-substrates-beyond-transformer-monoculture`,
`coil-attention-cyclic-memory-and-recurrence-contracts`,
`coilra-multicoil-rope-and-cyclic-mixers`

**Narrative job.** Make substrate choice a governed routing decision rather
than a permanent commitment to transformer monoculture or novelty-driven
architecture replacement.

**Central question.** How should a stack choose among general models,
specialists, recurrence, cyclic memory, search, tools, and future substrates?

**Argument moves.** Separate task and capability identities; route by qualified
fit, cost, authority, and recovery; compare heterogeneous substrates behind
stable fields; use Coil and CoilRA as concrete cyclic proposals without
claiming broad superiority; require matched migration and maintenance costs.

**Running example.** Route retrieval, patch generation, dependency analysis,
verification, and rollback checks to different qualified substrates while
preserving one task contract.

**Strongest objection.** Heterogeneous routing can add coordination cost and
correlated failure without outperforming one well-tuned general model.

**Evidence that would change the conclusion.** Matched natural tasks showing
no accepted-useful-output advantage after routing, migration, verification,
and maintenance costs would favor a simpler substrate strategy.

**Handoff.** Selected substrates still need a governed developmental process,
which Unit 20 treats as topology over learning rather than only weight updates.

**Target length.** 5,000-7,000 words.

## Unit 20 - Governed Training and Learning-Compute Topology

## Unit 21 - Adjudicated Persistence, Generalization, Feedback, Continual Learning, and Unlearning

## Unit 22 - Evaluation, Readiness, Thresholds, and Structured Assurance

**Lead owner.** `readiness-gates-residual-escrow-and-quarantine`

**Canonical owner routes.** `readiness-gates-residual-escrow-and-quarantine`,

`safety-cases-and-structured-assurance`,
`content-authenticity-watermarking-and-synthetic-media-integrity`

**Narrative job.** Turn evaluation into a repeated, adversarial, evidence-
compiled promotion decision rather than a benchmark score, safety label, or
one-time release checklist.

**Central question.** What evidence is sufficient to advance a capability when
the candidate may game tests, hide competence, corrupt provenance, or change
after evaluation?

**Argument moves.** Define readiness as explicit required checks and residual
custody; ratchet benchmarks against Goodhart pressure; trigger commitments at
capability thresholds; test sandbagging, scheming, and training-time deception;
compile claims-arguments-evidence safety cases; preserve content-authenticity
limits, expiry, quarantine, re-evaluation, and release separation.

**Running example.** Evaluate the changed repository system under hidden and
adversarial tests, discover a stale provenance claim, quarantine the candidate,
repair the safety case, and require readmission rather than score averaging.

performative compliance that optimize visible evidence while missing novel or
strategic failure.

detect deception, staleness, provenance failure, and deployment risk equally

**Handoff.** Evaluation consumes scarce computation and attention, so Unit 23

## Unit 23 - Generative Compression and Cognitive Resource Economics

**Narrative job.** Treat faster generation, compact models, artifact
compression, and test-time deliberation as competing allocations inside one

artifact, use a faster approximation, deliberate, verify, or fall back?

useful allocation.

## Unit 25 - Recursive Improvement, Replication, and Containment

**Lead owner.** `recursive-self-improvement-boundaries`

**Canonical owner routes.** `recursive-self-improvement-boundaries`,
`open-ended-improvement-engines`,
`autonomous-replication-proliferation-and-containment`

**Narrative job.** Show how a stack may generate, test, and adopt improvements
without letting proposal become ratification, local gain become descendant
authority, or replication escape containment and retirement.

**Central question.** What boundaries let a system improve recursively while
remaining interruptible, evidence-bound, and unable to authorize its own
proliferation?

**Argument moves.** Separate improvement proposal, evaluator, archive,
qualification, promotion, and rollback; preserve open-ended search diversity
without granting deployment; require versioned authority and descendant

and finite retirement; state bounded-liveness conditions and failure cases.

**Running example.** Let the stack propose a better verifier and workflow,

replacement, and prevent its artifacts from creating unauthorized descendants.

**Strongest objection.** Governance layers may be bypassed by a sufficiently
capable improver, while restrictive containment may prevent the very progress
the system is meant to enable.

**Evidence that would change the conclusion.** Competing architectures that
achieve equal improvement, reversibility, descendant control, and incident
recovery with fewer authority boundaries would justify simplification.

**Handoff.** The final unit must show the whole architecture operating as one
system and explain how Project Theseus and the Living Book keep it testable.

**Target length.** 5,500-7,500 words.
