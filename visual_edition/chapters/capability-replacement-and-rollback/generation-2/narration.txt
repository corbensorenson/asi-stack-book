A planning service moves from route A to route B. The candidate passes prechecks and enters a five-percent canary. Hours later, a delayed monitor finds a critical regression.

Can the system simply switch back?

A capable component is not a light bulb. It carries state and permissions. It may also have created effects outside the machine.

Before the canary begins, freeze the recovery point. Record the route and the candidate version. Inventory the state that must survive replacement.

The inventory includes the cache and policy. It also includes credentials and pending work. Each surface receives a recovery objective.

Route B enters the canary without permission to train descendants or change the default cache. The boundary limits how far a bad replacement can spread.

When the regression appears, promotion stops. The system restores route A from the prospectively chosen point.

Now compare each declared surface. The local cache matches. The policy matches. The pending queue can replay.

One remote service already received a message from route B. Switching the model back cannot unsend it.

This is the difference between rollback and compensation. Rollback restores a retained state. Compensation creates a new effect that addresses an irreversible one.

The replacement receipt therefore cannot say only "restored." It records the failed candidate and the recovered surfaces. It also keeps the remote message as a residual with an owner.

Suppose route B had been allowed to train a descendant during the canary. Would restoring route A remove that descendant?

No. Replacement must reach every affected branch, or explicitly admit that the branch remains outside recovery.

The evaluator and rollback authority should also be separated from the candidate. A component cannot be its own sole promoter and incident judge.

Repeated promotion attempts have costs. A cycle that always returns to safety may still accumulate external effects and operator burden.

Finite local campaigns can demonstrate exact restoration for a declared inventory. They cannot prove that the inventory was complete or that production behavior recovered.

The delayed regression makes the recovery test concrete. The transaction succeeds only when it distinguishes what was restored from what was compensated, contained, or still unresolved. A green restart is not the same as complete recovery.
