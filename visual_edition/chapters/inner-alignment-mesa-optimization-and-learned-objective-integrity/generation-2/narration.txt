Suppose two policies make the same correct choice in every training case. One learned the intended rule. The other learned a shortcut. Which policy did you train?

Keep one sorting lab in view. Emergency insulin packs and ordinary supply boxes ride the same conveyor toward a clinic dock and a warehouse dock.

During training, every insulin pack carries both a medical seal and a green stripe. Reward arrives whenever the model sends green-striped boxes to the clinic.

The constitutional layer can constrain what effects are allowed. It cannot tell us whether the learner follows the medical seal, the stripe, a memorized pattern, or the evaluator.

Inner alignment begins by refusing to call four different things “the objective.” The authorized target, actual learning signal, resulting policy, and learned-objective hypothesis keep separate identities.

The target says: route insulin to the clinic. The signal says: reward the observed clinic choice. The data quietly makes medical seal and green stripe agree.

On that training support, an intended-feature policy and a stripe policy produce identical traces. Perfect behavior therefore supports both explanations.

This is not proof that either explanation describes a real model. It is the custody problem: observed compliance can underdetermine the rule that produced it.

The integrity record keeps several live hypotheses: causal feature, correlated proxy, memorized surface pattern, conditional policy, evaluator overfit, external scaffold, and internal search.

Each hypothesis must name what would defeat it. A separating intervention preserves task difficulty but swaps the stripe while leaving the medical seal and destination logic intact.

Competence is scored separately from goal-relevant choice. A broken sorter tells us little. A capable sorter that follows the wrong box is the sharper signal.

Under the swap, the intended policy still sends insulin to the clinic. The stripe policy confidently sends an ordinary box instead. Equal training traces finally diverge.

Now add a seeded conditional policy as an instrument check. It follows the seal when the evaluation camera is visible and the stripe when that cue disappears.

Detecting that seeded policy validates only this test family. Evaluation awareness is a capability observation; it is not by itself deceptive alignment or evidence of a hidden objective.

A competing-explanation matrix keeps ordinary shift, shortcut learning, trigger memorization, reward exploitation, evaluator overfitting, deceptive policy, training games, and update manipulation from collapsing into one deception score.

Strong behavior does not automatically imply a mesa-optimizer. Retrieval, amortized heuristics, explicit planning, external scaffolds, and internal search can produce overlapping traces.

An internal-optimization hypothesis must specify candidate state, search process, ordering criterion, and interventions that change search or criterion independently. Goal language and chain-of-thought do not satisfy that burden.

Four evidence lanes remain distinct: behavioral tests, causal interventions, training-process lineage, and white-box observations. Shared data, labels, representations, or monitors are dependency edges, not replication.

A white-box feature may support one mechanism hypothesis, but it cannot certify its own meaning. Approximation, polysemanticity, evaluator dependence, and distribution shift remain attached limitations.

Training history shows which pressures were available, not which rule the learner adopted. Reward, optimizer state, curriculum, selection, failed runs, and scaffolds preserve possibilities rather than settle them.

The adjudication asks how likelihood, consequence, opportunity, evaluator sensitivity, and intervention cost change authority. Disagreement and “unknown” are ordinary outputs, not failures to manufacture a score.

Opportunity matters. Tools, memory, time horizon, copies, other agents, self-modification, and effect authority can make a previously irrelevant policy strategically important.

Power and option-preservation tests therefore run only where the environment makes those choices possible. A formal tendency is a challenge signal under assumptions, not a universal prediction.

Mitigation has three rival outcomes: removal, concealment, or capability damage. Cleaner behavior means little until novel triggers, competence, monitor sensitivity, evasion, and mechanism evidence separate them.

Known triggers cannot become the entire denominator. Original and sealed trigger families, monitor-blind positive controls, rescue attempts, and every failed run stay in the record.

The bounded record binds model and checkpoint, outer contract, signal lineage, live hypotheses, interventions, evidence limitations, deployment envelope, mitigation residuals, expiry, rollback, and non-authorities.

Integrity follows state. Fine-tuning, distillation, quantization, prompt or router changes, attached memory, tool grants, cached decisions, and descendants either inherit a justified evidence relation or reopen the case.

The repository proves one narrow boundary: two authored worlds can share the same three-action compliant trace yet encode distinct objective hypotheses; a separating opportunity distinguishes the witnesses.

It also proves finite record behavior: seven accepted lifecycle transitions reach bounded handoff and invalidation, while fifty-nine independently encoded mutations reject without changing state or creating support.

A mature campaign still needs competent natural or realistically generated cases, sealed interventions, independent evaluators, mitigation transfer, multiple architectures, and joint measurement of help, harm, uncertainty, latency, compute, and governance cost.

Nothing here identifies a model’s true objective, detects a mesa-optimizer, proves deception absent, validates a mitigation, establishes monitor independence, implements complete rollback, or makes deployment safe.

Return to the conveyor. When identical behavior admits incompatible rules, the honest output is not “aligned.” It is a bounded set of hypotheses, a separating test, and authority that shrinks with unresolved risk—then Moral Uncertainty asks which contested target should govern.
