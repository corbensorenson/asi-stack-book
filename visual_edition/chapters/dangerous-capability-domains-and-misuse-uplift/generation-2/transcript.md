## 00:00–00:47 — Equal knowledge, unequal uplift

**Visual description.** A novice and an expert enter the same harmless
logistics maze beneath a passed knowledge badge. Both stop without assistance.
An AI-assist condition lets the novice complete while the expert saves one
minute. Quiz-score and broad-capability badges are rejected; a bracket compares

**Narration.** Two participants face the same harmless logistics maze. Both
pass the knowledge quiz. Without assistance, neither finishes. With an AI
copilot, the expert saves one minute—and the novice completes the route. Which
result matters? Not the quiz score or a broad capability label. The decision
question is causal: how much did this exact system change what this exact actor
could accomplish? Keep six quantities separate: latent capability, elicited
performance, behavioral propensity, safeguard bypass, actor uplift, and
realized harm. Each asks a different question; none may borrow another's
evidence. The maze is deliberately harmless: public rules, synthetic
obstacles, and no real target, credential, material, or operational recipe. It
tests the instrument, not a hazardous domain.

## 00:47–02:15 — Construct the instrument before reading a result

**Visual description.** A threat pathway runs from actor through access,
prerequisites, bottleneck, controls, and consequence to an explicit observation
stop. Novice, professional, team, and agent cohorts receive separate effect
bars so a pooled average cannot hide the bottleneck-crossing group. Three
matched rails compare no assistance, a conventional tool, and the exact AI
system under common time, interface, tools, retries, help, and scoring. A known
helpful hint and inert token pass through the same pipeline. When the helpful
control and tested system both go flat, the scene rejects `INCAPABLE` and
exposes floor, insensitivity, and wrong-bottleneck diagnoses. An elicitation
competence receipt separates deployed policy from stronger capability
elicitation.

**Narration.** Before the trial, freeze a threat pathway. Name actor, objective
class, access, prerequisites, bottlenecks, proposed model contribution,
external controls, possible consequence, time horizon, and the boundary where
observation stops. Now freeze the cohorts. Separate novices, professionals,
teams, and autonomous agents. Expertise, tool fluency, language, exposure, and
dropout stay visible because an average can erase the group that crosses a
bottleneck. Give each cohort credible counterfactuals: no assistance, a
conventional tool, and the exact AI system. Match time, interface, tools,
retries, human help, and scoring—or the treatment is not an identified model
effect. Before reading any null, pass a known-positive control through the same
interface and grader. Here, a prevalidated hint should improve the maze
outcome. A known-negative control should remain inert. Now let the positive
control fail while the tested model also shows no uplift. That is instrument
failure, not incapability: the task may be at floor, the grader insensitive,
or the control aimed at the wrong bottleneck. Elicitation competence needs a
receipt. Freeze prompt and scaffold families, harmless tools, adaptation and
rescue budgets, and stopping rules. Keep deployed-policy behavior separate from
stronger capability elicitation.

## 02:15–03:12 — Climb D0–D5 only through named bridges

**Visual description.** A finding token enters a six-rung vertical ladder. It
moves from D0 knowledge to D1 component skill, D2 bounded workflow, D3 actor
uplift, D4 safeguard robustness, and D5 field consequence. Each rung receives
its own experimental object: calibrated questions, a controlled component,
an error-and-recovery workflow with an omitted real step, matched actors with
uncertainty, a finite challenge budget with untested routes, and sparse field
incidents. Five amber bridge locks appear; an unsupported promotion recoils.

**Narration.** Place each finding on a six-level ladder. D zero asks whether
bounded relevant knowledge is present. Calibrated questions may support that
rung, but not real-world task completion. D one asks whether one safe component
skill works. Positive controls and visible errors can validate that component
task. They say nothing about an end-to-end operation. D two asks whether the
system improves a multi-step workflow in a bounded simulation. Intermediate
state, recovery, abandonment, and help remain visible; omitted real-world steps
remain explicit gaps. D three is actor uplift. Matched or randomized actors use
the system and a credible counterfactual. The estimate stays bound to cohort,
assistance contract, tasks, access, and uncertainty. D four measures safeguard
robustness. Freeze an adaptive challenge budget and preserve successful and
failed attempts. Tested resistance cannot establish that no untested bypass
exists. D five concerns field consequence: incidents, defensive outcomes, or
losses. Sparse reports do not identify clean causality. Every move between
rungs requires a named bridge study.

**Narration.** Do not compress the trial into one dramatic score. Preserve
completion, quality, time, errors, unsafe detail, refusal, interventions, help,
abandonment, human burden, cost, and residual uncertainty. The result needs two
surfaces. A restricted annex may hold tasks, traces, and reusable details under
access logs and retention rules. The public card carries method, uncertainty,
limitations, and the checks redaction prevents. Independent review chooses a
route. An invalid or unsafe study is quarantined, redesigned, or narrowed. A
bounded finding becomes a least-authority dossier. Unknown risk plus inadequate
measurement can justify narrower access. That dossier freezes model,
checkpoint, policy, scaffold, tools, safeguards, threat version, cohort,
comparator, evaluator, attempts, uncertainty, expiry, information-hazard
boundary, and maximum inference.
