A living technical book is not a document that changes often. It is a system that can change without losing its source identity, claim history, failed tests, or public truth. New papers arrive, chapters split, proof targets move, support can strengthen or weaken, and derivatives drift. A static PDF freezes one moment. A loose wiki changes without enough discipline. A code repository can validate shape without explaining the thesis. The ASI Stack therefore treats each substantive change as an Evidence-Preserving Publication Transaction over a governed artifact graph.

The transaction first freezes the objective, canonical commit, affected chapters and claims, audience, authority, rights, release target, environment, time, and explicit non-claims. Stable identities bind sources, passages, claims, proof targets, tests, figures, derivatives, releases, residuals, corrections, and successor work. Intake keeps source report, author intent, synthesis, experiment, formal proof, editorial judgment, and open question in separate lanes. A source count does not become evidence. A theorem build does not become enforcement. A polished paragraph does not become support.

Each material claim is then atomized enough to name its scope, falsifier, evidence lanes, source mappings, contrary evidence, disposition, promotion ceiling, residual, and consumers. A typed change packet records what will move and why. Canonical records regenerate the outline, proof manifest, appendices, status surfaces, and reader projections. Validation is surface-specific: schema, semantics, sources, proofs, executables, negative controls, rights, accessibility, render, browser, release, reproducibility, deployment, and public observation remain different gates. Green is meaningful only for the contract that produced it.

Derivatives are projections, not rival books. The AI and research view, Human view, reader manuscript, HTML, DOCX, EPUB, PDF, article, header image, audio, and chapter videos all bind the exact source commit, audience, transform policy, review state, approval, evidence effect, residuals, and non-claims. Generation, inspection, accessibility review, editorial approval, release approval, deployment, public observation, archive, and external publication are distinct states. A smooth synopsis may compress navigation, but it cannot absorb specialist claim ownership. A rendered video may explain a chapter, but cannot promote it.

Now consider a correction. A source passage is withdrawn, so its mapping changes. The dependency graph identifies the affected claim, chapter prose, proof commentary, appendix rows, synopsis, transcript, video packet, and public page. The claim may be downgraded while the old release remains immutable and visibly historical. The current derivative becomes stale rather than silently current. Validators can catch missing regeneration and identity drift, but a human or separately authorized editorial process still owns interpretation, quality, rights, accessibility approval, and release. The correction closes only when every reachable consumer is updated, withdrawn, superseded, or held as an owned residual.

The repository demonstrates portions of this method: generated scaffolds, machine-readable manifests, source and claim ledgers, schemas, finite Lean models, executable negative controls, changelog lineage, commit-bound builds, deployed-page crawls, and derivative custody. The strongest integrated bundle contains three versioned lifecycle slices, twelve cases, all ten modeled lifecycle states, six observed effects, three exact rollbacks, a partial-effect residual and quarantine, an eleven-surface sealed epoch, twenty boundary injections, and eleven rejecting mutations. That is substantial local publication discipline. It is not proof that every source interpretation is correct, every chapter is true, every format is accessible, or the method transfers to another project.

The core remains Design rationale at argument support. Its maximum inference is bounded local replay only: no deployment, whole-book proof, external effect authority, transfer, publication, or release claim follows from the chapter. The book is part of its own argument, but self-application is not self-validation. The operational test is whether a future agent can discover what is canonical, what changed, what passed, what failed, what is public, what remains blocked, and which single roadmap has authority—without private memory. The next chapter, Open Research Agenda and Bibliography Plan, turns those unresolved obligations into a durable backlog for reading, building, testing, proving, reproducing, or rejecting.
