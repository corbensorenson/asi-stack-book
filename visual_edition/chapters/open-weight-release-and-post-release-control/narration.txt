This chapter asks a specific question: Once unrestricted model weights are published, the original releaser generally cannot recall copies, enforce serving-layer safeguards, observe use, or prevent derivatives. This irreversible transition has different control semantics from theft prevention and restricted custody. The tempting shortcut is insufficient: A model card, default refusal behavior, license, checksum, takedown request, or pre-release benchmark cannot control post-release fine-tuning, redistribution, merging, quantization, hidden deployment, or cumulative ecosystem effects.

The chapter's core claim is this: An open-weight release should require a prospective irreversible-release case that binds the exact artifact and license to accessible-frontier comparison, malicious-fine-tuning and scaffolded elicitation, marginal and cumulative risk, benefit and access distribution, downstream safeguard portability, derivative lineage, incident channels, post-release measurement, and residual ownership; after release, governance may inform, patch, coordinate, and support safer derivatives, but it must not claim revocation authority it no longer possesses. Classify access tiers and make the irreversible transition from custody to public replication explicit. Evaluate the exact candidate, competent maliciously fine-tuned variants, strong scaffolds, and the current accessible frontier under matched budgets.

Freeze exact bytes and comparators; test default and deliberately safety-removed variants; record license, documentation, signatures, derivative metadata, incident channels, patch semantics, and the limits of recall.
