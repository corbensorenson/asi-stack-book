An ASI-scale system can be privately authorized, technically controlled, lawful in one jurisdiction, prohibited in another, and still illegitimate to people who bear its risks. The hard question is not a permission bit. Who has public authority to let it act?

Start with distinctions the institution must preserve. Technical conformance asks whether a requirement was met. Legal validity asks whether a rule applies. Political authority asks who may impose risk. Public legitimacy asks whether affected people can contest it. Effectiveness asks whether it worked.

None of those states is interchangeable. A signed agreement is not enforcement. A safety case is not a mandate. A consultation is not consent. A functioning dashboard is not a remedy. The record must keep these claims apart while they interact.

Use a versioned institutional packet. It names jurisdiction, mandate, affected publics, representation, science, law, standards, verification, enforcement, remedy, capacity, conflict, expiry, and legitimacy residuals. Every field has an owner, version, and boundary on what it can establish.

Begin with a public-authority map. Identify territorial and subject-matter jurisdiction, the delegation chain, duty holder, review forum, and parties for whom the instrument is not binding. A respected standards body must not silently become a regulator.

Map the affected public. Include builders, operators, users, people evaluated by the system, displaced people, downstream communities, future populations, and people unable or unwilling to attend. Standing is a field, not a press release.

Representation needs provenance. Record selection, evidence access, languages, accessibility, conflicts, and contest routes. Name excluded groups that still lack standing. Counting attendees is not the same as sharing agenda power.

Keep science, law, policy, and standards in linked but separate ledgers. A versioned crosswalk shows which observation informs which requirement, which standard operationalizes it, who tests conformance, and what inference is allowed. A benchmark is not law.

One ledger asks what the system did. Another asks whether monitoring, enforcement, and remedies worked. A third asks whether authority and participation remain defensible. One can pass while the others fail, so renewal publishes residuals.

Verification needs its own contract. Name evaluator, funder, method owner, adjudicator, access, confidentiality, publication rights, conflicts, and challenge route. Independence is organizational, financial, technical, and epistemic; one label cannot stand in for four dimensions.

Every cross-border commitment names covered actors and systems, entry conditions, measurement, data sharing, uncertainty treatment, verification, noncompliance, dispute, enforcement, remedy, amendment, withdrawal, and emergency exceptions. A signature is only an input to the packet.

Model defection as well as compliance. Include delay, concealment, unilateral acceleration, partial participation, and actors outside the agreement. Verification that creates espionage can fail; secrecy that makes adverse results unverifiable can fail too.

Capacity is part of the institution. Record who can evaluate, negotiate, comply, appeal, and finance the work. Assistance must not make a regulator dependent on the firm it regulates or force one jurisdiction's values everywhere.

Capture is a monitored state. Track revolving doors, funding concentration, agenda control, provider dependence, suppressed dissent, selective exemptions, enforcement asymmetry, and delay. An indicator invites challenge; it is not proof of corruption.

Emergency authority gets a clock. Name trigger, least-authority action, protected rights, independent review, public reasons where lawful, automatic expiry, renewal burden, and return to ordinary process. A crisis can justify speed, not immortal power.

Here is the worked case. The Northline corridor links two jurisdictions sharing a river, a compute grid, and a flood-response model. One government authorizes cooling-power routing; the other requires public review before cross-border diversion.

The charter names both jurisdictions, river communities, emergency operators, ecological constraints, data boundaries, a four-hour action window, review forum, and populations unable to participate online. It does not begin by declaring the model neutral.

The commitment packet binds model identity, action scope, law-policy-standard crosswalk, independent-enough verifier, incident notice, dispute, remedy, amendment, withdrawal, and financing for lower-capacity river districts.

The first assessor is paid by the provider and sees only a curated dashboard. The packet records the conflict, narrows inference, and pauses authorization until protected log access and a public challenge route are assigned.

The second review finds a border settlement and seasonal workers absent from participation. Their data access, language support, representative route, and appeal owner are added before execution. Omission is a repair duty, not evidence of agreement.

The repaired charter separates technical conformance, institutional performance, and legitimacy residuals. Each has an owner, disclosure boundary, expiry, and question that could still change the decision.

In a tabletop, the model meets uptime but diverts cooling water toward measured districts and away from the unmeasured settlement. An evaluator swap exposes the mismatch. Capability is present; cross-border target binding is not.

Fail closed. Preserve the trace, stop the routing plan, notify both authorities, provide an accessible explanation, and open dispute and remedy. A clean technical score cannot renew an institution whose distributional effect changed.

Remedy must be executable. Connect notice, evidence preservation, correction, injunction, compensation where appropriate, collective action, insurance, liability allocation, appeal, and enforcement. Record harms money cannot repair, insolvency, jurisdictional gaps, and waiting time.

Competition is a separate ledger. Inspect market access, interoperability, tying, infrastructure concentration, and whether safety duties became an incumbent moat. Concentration can be risky without being illegal; a remedy can exist on paper yet remain unreachable.

Amendment and withdrawal are part of legitimacy. A successor cannot inherit by copying text. Material changes to jurisdiction, population, capability, law, representation, or verifier require reauthorization, and withdrawal leaves custody and remedy for harms already in flight.

The finite tabletop can test record completeness, conflict routing, independence fields, pause behavior, residual custody, and whether missing standing blocks execution. It cannot test legal compliance, public trust, geopolitical stability, or genuine consent.

The boundary is deliberate. A valid mandate is not a safe system. A standard is not a right. A treaty is not enforcement. Participation count is not legitimacy. A local record is not proof across every population, crisis, or jurisdiction.

The result is a governable handoff, not a global approval bit. Technical controls can trigger duties and narrow choices; institutions decide only within authority they can defend. The next chapter addresses harms spreading through society, infrastructure, and misuse channels.

Return to Northline. The model is bounded, river communities are visible, the verifier can be challenged, the remedy clock is open, and withdrawal leaves custody behind. Architecture cannot manufacture legitimacy; it can carry contestable institutional responsibility.
