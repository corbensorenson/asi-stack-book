"Use my private launch notes, make this amazing, and publish it today." The finish line sounds clear.

Now watch several powers hitch a ride. The system must choose an audience. It may expose private material. It may spend money or contact other people. None of those choices was actually granted.

Should the system act, refuse, or ask about everything?

Begin by preserving the request exactly. Interpretation can change, but the person's words remain available for correction.

Then separate outcome from means. The outcome is a strong launch candidate today. Publication is one possible means, not part of the outcome itself.

The system proposes a bounded default: prepare a private draft and compare three options. It labels the assumed audience and leaves spending at zero.

The requester corrects the assumptions. Private notes may inform the draft but may not be quoted. Nothing may be published yet.

Those corrections become the first intent contract. It authorizes a private artifact. It does not authorize a public effect.

Now the planner produces a polished page and a publish step. The page fits the contract. The publish step reaches the boundary and stops.

Could the planner call publication part of "making it amazing" and continue?

No. A material change must return to the person as a visible difference. The system cannot widen authority by paraphrasing the goal.

A later contract might name one channel and one reviewed artifact. It could also add an expiry and a revocation path.

Even then, the requester cannot grant rights that belong to other people. Privacy and ownership remain separate gates.

This boundary should not interrogate every harmless request. Friction should rise with consequence. Reversible drafting can proceed under explicit defaults. External effects need stronger confirmation.

Downstream tools must preserve the same ceiling. A plan or job cannot drop the prohibition on quoting private notes. A replacement model cannot inherit publication permission that never existed.

If the audience or source changes, the old contract may no longer fit. The same is true when spending or affected parties change.

Local schemas can show how these fields and transitions remain visible. They do not prove that a system understands natural language or that consent is valid in deployment.

In the original request, the person still receives useful work today. The system drafts privately, exposes its assumptions, and keeps publication behind a corrigible decision instead of treating desire as ambient power.
