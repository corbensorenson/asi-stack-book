# Storyboard — Human Intent as a Formal Input, generation 2

Video ID: `asi-video-human-intent-as-a-formal-input`

Teaching promise: the viewer can separate a desired outcome from permission,
predict which assumptions may support reversible drafting, and identify when a
material change must return to a versioned intent boundary before effects.

Target duration: 281.345 seconds. Narration: 623 words at 132.86 words per
minute. Format: 1920 × 1080, 30 fps, no music or sound effects in the locked
generation.

## Visual thesis

A fluent request becomes governable only when its visible outcome is separated
from the shadow assumptions and authority that would otherwise hitch a ride
into action. One launch request remains the continuous worked object. It moves
through a request ribbon, assumption tethers, a prediction fork, an intent
prism, correction diff, versioned contracts, a receipt, an authority gate, and
a downstream preservation chain.

Signature image: the request reaches two different endpoints. A private draft
is delivered through contract V1; publication remains behind an explicit V2
gate. The raw request stays visible, so neither branch can impersonate the
person's complete preference.

## Experience criteria

- The first two beats use clause-level timing so the request and attached powers
  change while their phrases are spoken; no six-second mechanical freeze remains.
- Captions occupy the lower safe region. Essential lifecycle and evidence labels
  stay above it, and the mechanism never depends on bottom text alone.
- The request, contracts, receipt, and authority gate recur across the arc. Scene
  changes therefore reframe the same causal story rather than substitute a card
  deck.
- Green always means bounded useful movement, never truth or general safety.
  Amber gates are duplicated by geometry and labels; magenta blocks end in
  stable crossed positions.
- The scorecard remains non-aggregate. No favorable average may hide an
  unauthorized action, missed help, privacy failure, or excessive burden.

## Evidence boundary

This visual explains a design rationale at `argument` support. The repository's
schemas, finite route models, synthetic intake cases, and rejecting mutations
test their encoded boundaries only. They do not establish natural-language
understanding, valid consent, user satisfaction, affected-party authorization,
deployed enforcement, safe effects, transfer, state of the art, AGI, or ASI.
