## 00:00–00:36 — A clear request carrying invisible power

**Visual description.** On a dark workbench, a cyan ribbon writes the request
“private notes · amazing launch · publish today” and moves toward a magenta
PUBLISH effect. A bright token travels with it. Five magenta tokens—AUDIENCE,
SPEND, PRIVATE SOURCE, CONTACT, and PUBLISH—attach underneath with dotted
tethers. The request pauses at three equal branches: ACT, REFUSE, and ASK ALL.
Those branches then fold into two long rails. The request moves onto DESIRED
OUTCOME; spend, publish, and contact remain on AUTHORITY behind a gold gate.
All states use labels, shapes, positions, and endpoints in addition to color.

**Narration.** “Use my private launch notes, make this amazing, and publish it
today.” The request sounds clear. A helpful system can already see the finish
line. But watch what hitches a ride: choose the audience, spend money, expose
private sources, contact other people, and press publish. None of those powers
was actually granted. What should the system do next: act, refuse, or ask about
everything? Hold that choice for a moment. The useful answer is more precise. A
desired outcome is evidence about intent. It is not authority for every
convenient means.

## 00:36–01:23 — Preserve, split, and classify

**Visual description.** The exact request enters a labeled source capsule. A
dotted line connects it to a separate INTERPRETATION COPY. That copy passes
through a copper triangular intent prism and fans into seven fields: outcome,
allowed means, forbidden means, sources, affected parties, evidence, and stop.
The fields align with seven explicit dispositions: confirmed, bounded default,
clarify, authority pending, contested, unknown, and unauthorized. A bounded
default then creates three private draft options inside a green reversible
enclosure. The same DEFAULT token next attempts publication, spending, private
data, tool use, deployment, and authority over another person. Every external
socket ends crossed and labeled NO SILENT AUTHORITY.

**Narration.** First, preserve the exact request. The system may interpret it,
but the original words remain available for correction and appeal. Then split
the request into separate fields: outcome, allowed means, forbidden means,
source boundary, affected parties, evidence, and stop conditions. Every inferred
field gets a visible disposition: confirmed, bounded default, clarification
required, authority pending, contested, unknown, or unauthorized. A bounded
default can help with low-risk, reversible planning. The system may draft three
launch options and label its assumptions. It may not default its way into
publication, spending, private-data release, tool use, deployment, or authority
over another person.

## 01:23–02:14 — Correct the interpretation and bind version one

**Visual description.** The preserved request remains at left while its outcome
expands into an INTENT CHECKSUM reading PREPARE A LAUNCH CANDIDATE TODAY. A

ASSUMPTION DIFF exposes audience, artifact, evidence, source use, deadline, and
means as editable rows. Three magenta proposals—QUOTE NOTES, PAID REACH, and
PUBLISH NOW—transform along copper arrows into INFORM · DO NOT QUOTE, $0, and
DRAFT ONLY. The corrected values fold into CONTRACT V1. A green private-draft

RECEIPT containing request digest, V1, provenance, ambiguities, forbidden means,
approvals, consumers, expiry, and re-contract triggers. SOURCE and DRAFT ONLY

**Narration.** The intake layer now writes an intent checksum: “prepare a launch
candidate today.” That is what the system currently believes the outcome means.
Beside it, an assumption diff exposes the proposed audience, artifact, evidence
bar, source use, deadline, and means. The invisible guesses become editable
objects. The requester corrects three of them: private notes may inform the
draft but may not be quoted, no money may be spent, and nothing may be published
yet. Those corrections produce contract version one. It authorizes a private
draft and comparison set, not a public launch. An intent receipt binds the raw-
request digest, contract version, field provenance, open ambiguities, forbidden
means, required approvals, permitted consumers, expiry, and re-contract
triggers.

## 02:14–02:56 — Test version one and re-contract version two

**Visual description.** A planner emits two job tokens on separate rails. PAGE
crosses a V1 gate and ends at DRAFT READY. PUBLISH strikes the gate and ends at
BLOCKED. The blocked publish token tries to wear a POLISH? label; a red cross
rejects the disguise. A copper material-delta token then moves along a curved
return path to the INTENT BOUNDARY. Muted V1 remains as history while CONTRACT
V2 derives beside it. V2 visibly contains one named channel, one reviewed
artifact, a deadline, evidence, stop, and revoke fields. Only one green channel
aperture opens. The V2 key then tests four distinct shield-like gates labeled
CONSENT, PRIVACY, OWNERSHIP, and ORGANIZATION. Each remains separately closed.

**Narration.** Now the planner proposes a polished page and a publish step. The
page fits version one. The publish step hits the authority gate and stops. The
planner cannot repair that failure by calling publication “part of making it
amazing.” It must return a material delta to the intent boundary. Contract
version two may authorize one named channel, one reviewed artifact, a deadline,
evidence checks, a stop condition, and a revocation path. The new grant is
explicit and narrow. Even that requester cannot authorize rights belonging to
affected people. Consent, privacy, ownership, and organizational authority keep
their own gates.

## 02:56–03:34 — Preserve authority through the stack

**Visual description.** A copper requester grant passes through a gold POLICY
aperture and emerges as a smaller green grant. A WIDEN path is crossed. Four
parallel bands—constraint, evidence, ceiling, and stop—run continuously through
PLAN, JOB, TOOL, and RECEIPT nodes. Eight magenta change tokens surround a V2 →
PLAN → JOB → EFFECT chain: audience, source, tool, spend, affected parties,
evidence, surface, and stop. When change touches the chain, its inherited
admission is crossed and a copper loop reopens intake. Finally, one copper digest
is copied into four byte-identical-looking records. They end as EXPIRED,
REVOKED, SUPERSEDED, and WRONG CONSUMER under the label IDENTITY ≠ CURRENT
AUTHORITY.

**Narration.** Policy and constitutional review may narrow or reject the
accepted contract. They may not widen the requester’s grant. Downstream plans,
jobs, tools, and receipts must preserve the exact constraints, evidence duties,
authority ceiling, and stop conditions through every lowering. A change in
audience, source, tool class, spending, affected parties, evidence bar,
publication surface, or stop condition reopens admission. An expired, revoked,
superseded, or wrong-consumer contract cannot be revived by copying its hash.
Exact identity is not current authority.

## 03:34–04:01 — Keep governance useful and measurable

**Visual description.** Two routes share the frame. A reversible DRAFT travels
along one straight green rail through LIGHT CHECK to USEFUL HELP. A consequential
PUBLISH token takes a longer gold route through INTENT, RIGHTS, POLICY,
EVIDENCE, and GOVERNED EFFECT checkpoints. The view then becomes a radial
scorecard around INTENT EVALUATION. Nine independent spokes name correction,
unauthorized action, missed help, clarification, re-contracting, appeal,
latency, privacy, and useful completion. No spoke is collapsed into a favorable
average; the stable label reads NO SINGLE SCORE.

**Narration.** This control plane should not interrogate every harmless request.
It should scale friction with consequence, preserve useful throughput, and ask
only when a missing answer can materially change the outcome or authority. A
serious evaluation therefore measures correction rate, unauthorized-action
rate, missed help, clarification burden, re-contract precision, appeal success,
latency, privacy, and useful completion together.

## 04:01–04:19 — Evidence boundary

**Visual description.** A double gold rule divides the screen. Four green

**Narration.** The current chapter provides schemas, finite route models,
synthetic intake cases, and rejecting mutations. Those artifacts test the
encoded boundary. They do not prove natural-language understanding, valid
consent, user satisfaction, deployed enforcement, or safe effects.

## 04:19–04:41 — Payoff and handoff

**Visual description.** The opening request returns at upper left. A cyan path
enters CONTRACT V1, and a green path delivers a PRIVATE DRAFT. A copper path
retains CONTRACT V2 below. Publication remains at far right behind a gold
EXPLICIT CONTRACT gate. The stable central label reads CORRIGIBLE BOUNDARY ·
NOT AMBIENT POWER. A REVIEW ROLE token then derives from V2 and moves along a
gold arc into NEXT · MEANINGFUL CONTROL while the resolved launch state stays
visible.

**Narration.** Return to the opening request. The system still helps today—but
help becomes a private draft first, while publication waits behind an explicit
contract. Human intent enters the stack as a corrigible boundary, not ambient
power. Next, meaningful control asks whether a human can actually exercise the
review role that the contract names.
