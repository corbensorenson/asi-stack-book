This chapter asks a specific question: The book needs to decide which architecture claims should become executable specs or Lean proofs. The tempting shortcut is insufficient: Formal methods are ineffective when applied to vague philosophical claims instead of operational predicates and transitions.

The design can still fail. Formal notation or theorem counts decorate an argument whose operational predicate and consumer use were never defined. A broad safety, alignment, capability, quality, deployment, or SOTA claim is hidden inside a trivial finite record predicate. A passing schema, process validator, behavior test, benchmark, or external citation is described as a Lean proof or equivalent formal guarantee. A direct projection or assumption restatement is marketed as a derived semantic invariant. The correct response is to stop, narrow, quarantine, compensate, or retain an owned residual rather than narrate uncertainty away.
