“Review required. Approve transfer?” The recommendation is green. A contradictory pressure reading is buried below it. Seven other alarms are waiting.

The system will commit in twelve seconds. Its stop path needs fourteen. A person is present, but is meaningful control still present?

What should happen: accept the approval, ask the reviewer to hurry, or put the process into safe hold? Keep your answer.

Automation often handles the routine cases and returns only the rare abnormal one to a person. The easy work disappears; context, practice, and time disappear with it.

So separate four ideas. A nominal approval is a click. An informed review adds usable evidence and task comprehension. An effective intervention adds authority, alternatives, time, and a working channel.

Meaningful control is the larger social and normative judgment. The stack must never infer it automatically from a green checklist, a fast response, or a confident operator.

Instead, begin with a versioned oversight contract. Name the task, consequence, operating mode, controller role, authority ceiling, evidence view, alternatives, intervention points, safe fallback, responsibility ceiling, and expiry.

For this episode, add a capacity snapshot: qualification scope, evidence digest, decision window, queue load, conflict state, accommodations, channel health, uncertainty, and missing fields.

This is a task record, not a score of the person. It may defeat reliance on a review role. It may not rank a worker, diagnose fatigue, or grant new authority.

Now test eight necessary conditions separately: authority, observability, qualification, time, load, intervention, reachable recovery, and conflict or incentive disposition.

The time test is brutally concrete. Start at irreversibility, then subtract notice, understanding, decision, action, system response, and a safety margin.

Here, twelve seconds remain, but the declared path needs sixteen. The margin is negative. The override button exists, yet effective intervention does not.

Load matters independently. Seven queued alarms and an interrupted reviewer can make approval batching likely, even when the interface and authority are otherwise correct.

Representation and authority also stay separate. A fluent recommendation can hide conflicting evidence, and responsibility is not legitimate when the named controller cannot veto, redirect, appeal, or reach safety.

Any missing or uncertain condition narrows the route: clarify, add capacity, slow the process, reduce autonomy, enter safe hold, or abstain and escalate.

The credible baseline is simple: risk tiers, fixed lead times, visible mode and consequence, an independent evidence view, a tested stop path, dual review at the highest tier, and hard queue limits.

An adaptive control envelope earns complexity only if it beats that baseline on joint outcomes without hiding false intervention, delay, unequal burden, privacy cost, or missed useful work.

Repair the transfer episode. Stage the action reversibly. Freeze the evidence view. Put the active mode, contradiction, alternatives, and last reversible point in one compatible representation.

Extend the window and ask one task-relevant comprehension question: which signal conflicts with the recommendation, and what alternative preserves a reachable safe state?

Then exercise the intervention path. The reviewer selects safe hold; the control channel responds inside its declared latency; independent observation confirms that the transfer never committed.

The receipt records exactly what was shown, the timing, the choice, the intervention request, the system response, the residual, and the appeal or recovery owner.

Responsibility stays bounded by recorded control. A click cannot erase missing evidence, impossible timing, coercive pressure, failed intervention, disagreement, or an unavailable alternative.

And the cure must not become surveillance. Prefer task and system conditions, voluntary coarse readiness, and privacy-minimized records—not cameras, emotion inference, keystroke scoring, or concealed worker profiles.

A material change to the task, model, interface, evidence view, consequence, authority, reviewer role, time window, or intervention path expires the old admission.

Evaluate the design without one flattering score: defect detection, correct and false intervention, missed defects, calibrated reliance, useful throughput, burden, privacy, unequal impact, latency, and recovery all remain visible.

The repository currently proves only finite record routing: schemas, a safe-hold fixture, rejecting mutations, and a bounded formal model. It proves no comprehension, workload threshold, human efficacy, responsibility, deployment, safety, or transfer.

Return to the opening choice. Safe hold was correct because the known control envelope had already failed—not because the system measured the reviewer’s worth. The rebuilt episode supplies useful control without responsibility theater.
