# Storyboard — Human Factors and Meaningful Control in Oversight, generation 2

## Teaching promise

The viewer can distinguish a nominal approval from an exercisable control
relationship and predict when missing time, information, authority, capacity,
or intervention must force safe hold rather than responsibility theater.

## Visual thesis

The film stays inside one synthetic transfer-control episode. A green
recommendation, buried pressure contradiction, overloaded reviewer, two clocks,
eight-segment control envelope, stop channel, safe-state basin, and intervention
receipt recur as causal objects. The recurring question is not whether a person
is present; it is whether that person has a usable evidence view, enough time,
bounded authority, a reachable alternative, and an exercised channel before the
action becomes irreversible.

The signature image is a reviewer inside an incomplete eight-segment envelope.
TIME, LOAD, and STOP remain open in magenta while the same transfer episode
moves into a green safe-state basin. A gold shield labeled NO PERSON SCORE keeps
the task-level routing decision visually separate from worker judgment.

## Art direction

- Dark blue-black control-room field with restrained cyan, copper, gold, green,
  and magenta semantic accents from the visual-edition grammar.
- Rounded operational objects, hard double-rule evidence boundaries, segmented
  circular control geometry, and physical tokens moving through explicit paths.
- Green denotes a bounded route that completed its declared condition—not truth,
  competence, general safety, or meaningful control.
- Magenta failures terminate in open segments, crosses, or stop bars. Gold denotes
  authority or normative boundaries. Every state is duplicated by text, shape,
  position, endpoint, or line style.
- The lower caption-safe band remains free of indispensable labels. No dense
  paragraph card substitutes for the narrated mechanism.

## Audio and synchronization

- Final narration duration: 347.060 seconds at approximately 125.9 words per
  minute. The narration master is the clock authority.
- Beat boundaries are derived from the exact 87-cue caption receipt. The opening
  recommendation, buried contradiction, and seven-alarm reveal use clause-level
  timing at 00:03.643 and 00:07.226.
- The 12-second versus 16-second counterexample, baseline completion, exercised
  hold path, evidence boundary, and opening callback each end in a stable visual
  result before the next causal move begins.
- Music and decorative effects remain absent until narration-only clarity and
  exact sync pass. No sound effect is allowed to carry semantic information.

## Experience criteria

- The reviewer, episode, segmented envelope, clock, stop channel, safe basin,
  and receipt recur across the arc; the film must read as one causal episode,
  not a sequence of interchangeable cards.
- No operational measurement crosses the double-rule boundary into a
  declaration of meaningful control.
- SAFE HOLD is shown as useful bounded control, not a universal policy or proof
  of safety. The static baseline is visually credible before the adaptive design
  is evaluated.
- Random-frame review must preserve a single dominant relation and readable
  labels at phone size. Captions must not cover essential state labels.
- The final frame must answer the opening prediction and retain the next
  chapter's communication vulnerability without undoing the safe state.

## Evidence boundary

This visual explains a design rationale at `argument` support. Repository-local
schemas, a safe-hold fixture, rejecting mutations, and a bounded formal model
test finite record routing only. They do not establish reviewer comprehension,
a workload threshold, human efficacy, moral or legal responsibility,
deployment, safety, transfer, state of the art, AGI, or ASI.
