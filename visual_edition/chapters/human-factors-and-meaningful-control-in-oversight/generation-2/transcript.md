**Narration.** “Review required. Approve transfer?” The recommendation is green.
A contradictory pressure reading is buried below it. Seven other alarms are
waiting. The system will commit in twelve seconds. Its stop path needs fourteen.
A person is present, but is meaningful control still present? What should
happen: accept the approval, ask the reviewer to hurry, or put the process into
safe hold? Keep your answer. Automation often handles the routine cases and
returns only the rare abnormal one to a person. The easy work disappears;
context, practice, and time disappear with it.

## 00:39–01:43 — Separate the concepts and bind the episode

**Visual description.** Four independent rungs appear: NOMINAL · CLICK,
INFORMED · EVIDENCE, EFFECTIVE · CONTROL, and MEANINGFUL · NORMATIVE. CHECKLIST,
FAST, and CONFIDENT tokens next try to cross a double rule into MEANINGFUL? and
are stopped under NO AUTO-CERTIFICATION. The transfer episode folds inside a
copper OVERSIGHT CONTRACT · V1 ring while eleven task and control fields dock
around it. A dotted lineage edge derives a CAPACITY SNAPSHOT · EPISODE ONLY with
nine current-condition fields. The snapshot has one permitted route to DEFEAT
ROLE RELIANCE. Routes to RANK, DIAGNOSE, and GRANT end crossed beside TASK RECORD
· NOT PERSON SCORE.

**Narration.** So separate four ideas. A nominal approval is a click. An informed
review adds usable evidence and task comprehension. An effective intervention
adds authority, alternatives, time, and a working channel. Meaningful control
is the larger social and normative judgment. The stack must never infer it
automatically from a green checklist, a fast response, or a confident operator.
Instead, begin with a versioned oversight contract. Name the task, consequence,
operating mode, controller role, authority ceiling, evidence view, alternatives,
intervention points, safe fallback, responsibility ceiling, and expiry. For this
episode, add a capacity snapshot: qualification scope, evidence digest, decision
window, queue load, conflict state, accommodations, channel health, uncertainty,
and missing fields. This is a task record, not a score of the person. It may
defeat reliance on a review role. It may not rank a worker, diagnose fatigue, or
grant new authority.

## 01:43–02:52 — Test the control envelope without averaging failures away

**Visual description.** Eight labeled arcs grow around the reviewer: AUTH, OBS,
QUAL, TIME, LOAD, STOP, RECOVER, and CONFLICT. TIME, LOAD, and STOP remain open
and magenta. TIME unrolls into a horizontal budget for notice, understanding,
decision, action, response, and margin. A cyan 12-second bar is overrun by a
gold 16-second path; MARGIN −4s appears while an OVERRIDE BUTTON remains behind
a broken line labeled ≠ EFFECTIVE. Seven alarms stack beside an interrupted
reviewer and independently illuminate LOAD FAIL. A fluent recommendation then
peels open to conflicting evidence while VETO, REDIRECT, APPEAL, and SAFE STATE
keys remain crossed. Six narrower remedies fan from the failed envelope, and
the episode token travels into SAFE HOLD.

**Narration.** Now test eight necessary conditions separately: authority,
observability, qualification, time, load, intervention, reachable recovery, and
conflict or incentive disposition. The time test is brutally concrete. Start at
irreversibility, then subtract notice, understanding, decision, action, system
response, and a safety margin. Here, twelve seconds remain, but the declared
path needs sixteen. The margin is negative. The override button exists, yet
effective intervention does not. Load matters independently. Seven queued
alarms and an interrupted reviewer can make approval batching likely, even when
the interface and authority are otherwise correct. Representation and authority
also stay separate. A fluent recommendation can hide conflicting evidence, and
responsibility is not legitimate when the named controller cannot veto,
redirect, appeal, or reach safety. Any missing or uncertain condition narrows
the route: clarify, add capacity, slow the process, reduce autonomy, enter safe
hold, or abstain and escalate.

## 02:52–03:18 — Compete against a strong simpler baseline

**Visual description.** A straight static rail gains fixed checkpoints for risk
tier, lead time, visible mode and consequence, independent evidence, a tested
stop, dual review, and a queue limit. A transfer token traverses the completed
rail and reaches a safe-state basin. The static rail then shares the frame with
the adaptive envelope. Six independent outcome tiles—false stop, delay, unequal
burden, privacy, missed help, and useful work—remain visible under NO SINGLE
WINNER.

**Narration.** The credible baseline is simple: risk tiers, fixed lead times,
visible mode and consequence, an independent evidence view, a tested stop path,
dual review at the highest tier, and hard queue limits. An adaptive control
envelope earns complexity only if it beats that baseline on joint outcomes
without hiding false intervention, delay, unequal burden, privacy cost, or
missed useful work.

## 03:18–04:05 — Repair, exercise, and record the intervention

**Visual description.** The held transfer episode returns in a reversible
staging view. MODE · WAITING, the pressure contradiction, TRANSFER and SAFE HOLD
alternatives, and LAST REVERSIBLE POINT become adjacent. A gold TASK CHECK puts
the reviewer between the conflicting signal and both alternatives. SELECT HOLD
then travels through a green CHANNEL · WITHIN LATENCY into the safe-state basin.
INDEPENDENT · NOT COMMITTED appears only after the route completes. The result
unrolls into an INTERVENTION RECEIPT with seven provenance-linked cells: view,
timing, choice, request, response, residual, and owner.

**Narration.** Repair the transfer episode. Stage the action reversibly. Freeze
the evidence view. Put the active mode, contradiction, alternatives, and last
reversible point in one compatible representation. Extend the window and ask
one task-relevant comprehension question: which signal conflicts with the
recommendation, and what alternative preserves a reachable safe state? Then
exercise the intervention path. The reviewer selects safe hold; the control
channel responds inside its declared latency; independent observation confirms
that the transfer never committed. The receipt records exactly what was shown,
the timing, the choice, the intervention request, the system response, the
residual, and the appeal or recovery owner.

## 04:05–04:44 — Bound responsibility, privacy, and reuse

**Visual description.** A RESPONSIBILITY band attempts to move beyond the
receipt but meets a hard copper ceiling. Six residual anchors—missing evidence,
impossible time, pressure, failed stop, disagreement, and no alternative—force
the band to contract. A privacy wall next admits task state, system state, and
voluntary coarse readiness while CAMERA, EMOTION, KEYSTROKES, and HIDDEN PROFILE
remain crossed outside. Finally, an admitted eight-segment ring is surrounded
by nine material-change triggers. INTERFACE touches it, every segment expires,
and a return loop opens.

**Narration.** Responsibility stays bounded by recorded control. A click cannot
erase missing evidence, impossible timing, coercive pressure, failed
intervention, disagreement, or an unavailable alternative. And the cure must
not become surveillance. Prefer task and system conditions, voluntary coarse
readiness, and privacy-minimized records—not cameras, emotion inference,
keystroke scoring, or concealed worker profiles. A material change to the task,
model, interface, evidence view, consequence, authority, reviewer role, time
window, or intervention path expires the old admission.
