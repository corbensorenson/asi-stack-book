One emergency generator can keep either the Harbor Clinic's vaccine refrigerator or the Hillside Homes' cooling center alive tonight. The allocator has one slot, two urgent requests, and no honest way to call the conflict solved.

Both requests pass the safety screen. The clinic protects patients and vaccines. The homes protect older residents who cannot safely evacuate. The same available resource now points toward two defensible obligations.

If the system displays one total score and sends the generator to the clinic, should we call that answer fair? Hold the question open, because agreement about the action is not agreement about the value that selected it.

The tempting shortcut is a single reward number. It adds predicted lives saved, economic cost, public visibility, and schedule pressure until the conflict looks like ordinary ranking.

That ranking hides what the number cannot settle. Who has standing, which harms are reversible, whose consent matters, and what must remain contestable before the machine acts?

Moral uncertainty is not one missing coefficient. Safety, equity, autonomy, privacy, truthfulness, and institutional duty can all matter, and the people affected may reasonably disagree about precedence.

A majority vote does not repair the gap. A quiet minority, a future person, a third party, or someone unable to attend the meeting can disappear before the vote is counted.

The first engineering move is a value-conflict record. It keeps each proposition, its source, its uncertainty, its affected parties, its stakes, and the dissent that a summary would otherwise erase.

The record also names the decision procedure. It might use precedence, a veto, a reversible lottery, a tribunal, a negotiated compromise, abstention, or escalation, but it records who chose that procedure and what the procedure cannot prove.

A bounded decision lease then turns the unresolved conflict into a temporary permission. It names the allowed action, the prohibited action, the authority ceiling, the expiry, the revisit trigger, and the rollback or redress path.

The lease is not moral truth. It is a narrow authorization to do one thing for one scope while the disagreement remains visible and recoverable.

A linked rights receipt gives the affected people handles on that authorization. It records the audit artifact, explanation, dissent, appeal route, correction owner, timing, accessibility, cost, and receipt of each request.

Those handles need custody outside the challenged decision maker. If the same agent decides, stores the record, judges the appeal, and scores the outcome, polished transparency can still be capture.

Withholding is also a decision. A denial or redaction record names the reason, necessity, proportionality, withheld-material inventory, expiry, reviewer, appeal path, and later-disclosure trigger.

Now consider exit and export. Leaving the service is not meaningful if identity, memory, shared records, or dependent tools cannot move with enough semantic fidelity to preserve practical continuity.

Forking is not an unrestricted copy either. A safe fork carries source and license lineage, privacy and constitutional duties, unresolved conflict, revoked material, audit receipts, and a new qualification gate.

Run the Harbor decision through the lease. The proposal names both neighborhoods, the generator, the two safety constraints, the selected procedure, a four-hour expiry, and a promised review before the next allocation.

The first version fails before execution. It names the clinic and the homes, but it leaves the residents who share the cooling center's dependency outside the standing and notice fields.

That failure is not a moral verdict. It is a missing record obligation, so the gate stops the effect and sends the lease back for repair.

The repaired record adds the affected residents, their representative route, the medical and privacy constraints, and a dissent payload that survives the temporary choice.

The lease can now authorize a narrow action: deliver the generator to the clinic for four hours, keep the homes' cooling route open, and schedule an independent review before renewal.

The rights receipt travels with the action. It exposes the decision rule, the evidence view, the accountable owner, the appeal deadline, the export scope, and the residual uncertainty without claiming that anyone won the moral argument.

An independent-enough custodian can audit the record while the allocator continues its work. A redacted health detail remains counted, its digest remains visible, and its appeal route remains alive.

If the system is replaced, the receipt and the dissent cannot vanish with the model. A descendant inherits the lineage and must requalify whenever a conflict, jurisdiction, tool, or authority boundary changes.

The finite harness can check these records. It can accept a bounded transition, reject a missing standing field, preserve a dissent payload, and refuse an authority increase that lacks review.

Those checks do not settle moral truth. A valid lease is not a correct theory, a vote is not consent, and an aggregate score is not legitimacy.

They do not establish legal rights, independent review quality, usable export, safe forks, institutional adequacy, or deployed governance. Those are separate evidence lanes with separate owners.

The result is therefore useful but narrow: disagreement survives as state, authority stays bounded, and challenge paths remain available before the next effect. The unresolved moral burden is not hidden in a score.

The next chapter asks what happens after a governed stack has recorded a conflict and still needs an operational objective. Value learning must inherit the uncertainty rather than quietly pretending the lease was a consensus.

Return to the generator. The two neighborhoods still matter, the temporary delivery is visible, the dissent remains attached, and the next review is scheduled. A contestable system does not erase disagreement; it keeps disagreement governable.
