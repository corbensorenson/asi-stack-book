One emergency generator can serve either a clinic refrigerator or a neighborhood cooling center tonight. Both requests pass the safety screen. There is no honest way to call the conflict solved.

The clinic protects vaccines and patients. The cooling center protects older residents who cannot easily leave. One resource now points toward two defensible obligations.

Could a single score settle the choice fairly?

The score might combine predicted harm and economic cost. It could also include visibility or schedule pressure. But addition hides questions the numbers do not answer.

The real dispute concerns standing and repair. It also concerns consent and which parts of the decision must remain open to challenge.

Record the conflict before ranking it. Keep both propositions and the people they affect. Preserve the reasons for disagreement instead of compressing them into a confidence bar.

The system still needs an action. So it issues a temporary decision lease rather than declaring moral truth.

The lease names one allowed use of the generator and a short expiry. It also names the next review and a path for appeal or redress.

The first draft sends the generator to the clinic for four hours. Then the standing check reveals that residents who depend on the cooling center were omitted from notice and review.

The gate stops. This is not a verdict that the clinic should lose. It is a finding that the decision process left affected people outside the record.

Repair the lease. Add the residents and their representative route. Keep the cooling center's alternative path visible. Preserve the objection to the temporary choice.

Now the generator can move under bounded authority while the disagreement remains alive. A linked receipt travels with the action and names who must revisit it before renewal.

The appeal record should not be controlled only by the allocator. Otherwise the same actor can decide and judge the challenge.

What happens if the system is replaced overnight?

The dissent cannot vanish with the model. A successor inherits the unresolved record and must requalify the decision before extending it.

Finite checks can enforce required standing and reject an unauthorized widening. They cannot settle moral truth or prove that the review is legitimate in practice.

The generator decision leaves both the temporary delivery and the competing obligation visible. A contestable system does not pretend disagreement disappeared; it keeps the next decision open to the people who still bear it.
