This chapter asks a specific question: Behavioral tests can reveal what a model did without identifying the internal features, circuits, representations, or causal pathways that produced it, while interpretability methods can themselves generate persuasive but unstable stories. The tempting shortcut is insufficient: A probe, visualization, sparse feature label, attribution map, or circuit diagram is not white-box proof unless it is reproducible, causally tested, stable under relevant transformations, scoped to a model/version/data regime, and compared with plausible alternative explanations.

The chapter's core claim is this: Internal-state observations should enter governance only as typed evidence artifacts with lineage, method assumptions, replication status, causal interventions, stability checks, coverage limits, and explicit non-authority; white-box evidence complements but does not replace behavioral and operational evidence. Define an evidence packet containing model hash, dataset slice, method version, feature/circuit identifiers, assumptions, and confidence. Separate observational association, predictive decoding, causal necessity, causal sufficiency, and completeness claims. Require held-out replication across prompts, seeds, checkpoints, and relevant transformations.

A concrete implementation trace makes the proposal testable. The implemented minimum is a strict internal-evidence packet schema, one explicitly expired record-shape fixture, a separately implemented semantic consumer with twelve rejecting mutations, and a finite Lean route that rejects scientifically incomplete packets, expires changed packets, and cannot grant authority. The claim-bearing campaign is prospectively frozen and resource-isolated but unexecuted; it requires one digest-bound open model, two independently owned methods, a tuned behavioral baseline, positive and negative controls, causal interventions, stability and residual accounting, an independent evaluator, and a one-time held-out opening after seven competence gates.
