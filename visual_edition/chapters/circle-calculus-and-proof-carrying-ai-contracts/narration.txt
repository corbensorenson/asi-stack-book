This chapter asks a specific question: The stack needs a way to turn selected mathematical and AI-infrastructure claims into theorem-linked, machine-readable contracts without treating proofs as model-quality evidence. The tempting shortcut is insufficient: Ordinary prose, tests, and diagrams can show intent, but they do not give downstream systems stable theorem IDs, proof status, receipt fingerprints, replay checks, and explicit non-claims.

The chapter's core claim is this: Circle Calculus and Proof-Carrying AI Contracts owns a theorem-, model-, artifact-, implementation-, consumer-, claim-, version-, and time-specific Proof Contract Transport Envelope: a finite formal fact may travel only with resolvable proof identity, exact assumptions and semantics, source and toolchain provenance, content fingerprints, deterministic recomputation or replay, least-authority consumer gates, expiry and revocation, and preserved non-claims; theorem validity, receipt readiness, archive integrity, or transport success alone confers no model-quality, runtime, memory, safety, deployment, transfer, support, or SOTA authority. Register the exact formal claim, theorem namespace and identity, finite model, assumptions, proof status, source version, toolchain, implementation object, intended consumers, and prohibited inferences.

A concrete implementation trace makes the proposal testable. The exact current minimum contains two schema-valid proof/receipt records; one external clean-checkout Circle rope slice with a narrow non-core argument-to-prototype-backed transition; one ASI-side gate accepting one pinned receipt and rejecting four mutations with no core effect; one digest-verified archive of nine contracts and four policy receipts with five rejected controls and a no-change decision; and ten finite local Lean theorem declarations. It does not establish a locally built general resolver, end-to-end transport service, semantic refinement, descendant revocation, independent reproduction, downstream empirical benefit, or chapter-core transition.

The design can still fail. A theorem name, badge, diagram, proof count, or green build creates authority beyond the exact statement. A theorem ID resolves to the wrong declaration, changed statement, different assumptions, stale source, or incompatible toolchain. The formal model is correct but does not faithfully represent the engineering object or deployed implementation. Exact finite or discrete facts are applied to approximate, floating-point, probabilistic, continuous, or adversarial behavior without a bridge. The correct response is to stop, narrow, quarantine, compensate, or retain an owned residual rather than narrate uncertainty away.
