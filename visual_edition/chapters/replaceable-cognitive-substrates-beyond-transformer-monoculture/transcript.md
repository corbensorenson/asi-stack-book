## 00:00–00:42 — Monoculture lock-in

**Visual description.** A round socket labeled `TOKEN + KV CACHE` accepts a
Transformer block but rejects differently shaped SSM, recurrent, graph, and
program blocks. The socket expands into a rectangular `COGNITIVE KERNEL ABI`
whose contract fields are visible.

**Narration.** The Transformer is a powerful implementation, not the definition of cognition. Yet a stack can claim architectural freedom while baking token, attention-cache, positional, checkpoint, and serving assumptions into every surrounding layer. Then a state-space model, recurrent network, test-time learner, graph system, exact program, or future substrate is replaceable only on paper. This chapter proposes a typed Cognitive Kernel ABI: a boundary that lets routes use the best qualified implementation for an exact job while identity, authority, evidence, memory, cost, fallback, and recovery remain stable above it.

## 00:42–01:24 — Request and proposal

**Visual description.** A typed request enters the ABI with task, state,
authority, budget, stop, evidence, fallback, hardware, and time fields. The
return packet contains proposal, uncertainty, state delta, checkpoint, cost,
assistance, identity, and non-claims. A red stop bar blocks a direct path from
kernel to external effect.

**Narration.** The ABI is not a lowest-common-denominator forward function. A request binds task, consumer, modality, context handles, exact state handles, authority ceiling, resource budget, stop policy, required evidence, fallback, hardware, and time horizon. A kernel returns a proposal, uncertainty or residual, evidence handles, requested effects, requested authority, state delta, checkpoint, cost and assistance receipts, kernel identity, version, state schema, and non-claims. The kernel may propose an effect. It may not commit one, mint authority, forge a receipt, choose its only evaluator, or silently alter durable state.

## 01:24–02:07 — Different computational affordances

**Visual description.** Seven cards name Transformer, SSM/recurrent,
test-time learner, external memory, KAN, graph/message passing, and
diffusion/program/search. Each card shows one state affordance and one
checkpoint or memory obligation. No card is crowned.

**Narration.** This boundary preserves differences rather than pretending they do not exist. Transformers expose attention and key-value state. State-space and recurrent systems compress history into evolving state. Test-time-learning systems mutate parameters or fast memory during inference. External-memory controllers add addressable stores. KANs change function parameterization. Graph and message-passing systems encode relation structure. Diffusion-style generators revise parallel states. Programs and search can preserve exact symbolic branches. Each family has different checkpoint, deletion, calibration, hardware, memory, and failure semantics. If an adapter cannot preserve a required exact field, the route is rejected.

## 02:07–02:50 — Routing above substrate

**Visual description.** Reflex, reaction, deliberation, and specialist heads
route into qualified kernel cards. A contribution ledger separately lists
kernel, retrieval, tool, search, verifier, adapter, and hardware cost.

**Narration.** Above the ABI, reflex, reaction, deliberation, and specialist routers select candidates by qualified capability rather than fashion. A reflex may be a deterministic rule, small KAN, or recurrent cell. A streaming reaction may use an SSM. Deliberation may combine a general model, exact executor, learned critic, and branch-preserving search. Every receipt credits retrieval, tools, search, verifiers, adapters, and hardware. This prevents a tiny central model from receiving credit for intelligence supplied by a huge corpus, hand-authored skills, expensive voting, or hidden outer search. Keep-it-simple means the smallest complete governed system, not the smallest box in the diagram.

## 02:50–03:34 — Architectural RSI

**Visual description.** A candidate moves through propose, isolate, qualify,
compare, shadow, canary, promote or narrow, rollback, and retire. The evaluator
and approval gate remain separate from the candidate; failed descendants stay
visible.

**Narration.** Architectural recursive self-improvement is therefore more than shuffling weights. A system may propose a new computational structure, train it in isolation, test conformance and capability with independent-enough evaluators, compare strong baselines at equal active parameters and equal total lifecycle cost, shadow it without effect authority, canary it with fallback, and then promote, narrow, combine, quarantine, roll back, or retire it. Failed comparisons and descendants remain visible. A candidate can help design its successor, but cannot select the only data, threshold, evaluator, or promotion record used to approve itself.

## 03:34–04:18 — OneCell can lose

**Visual description.** A small recurrent cell iterates over typed latent lanes
beside an external exact-state store and branch-preserving search tree. Six
defeat cards surround it: adapter burden, answer-bearing assistance, hidden
memory, depth failure, lane interference, and matched comparator loss.

**Narration.** OneCell illustrates how a candidate should lose cleanly. It imagines a compact weight-tied recurrent cell over typed latent lanes, with exact memory, identity, effects, and receipts outside the cell; inner recurrence refines proposals while outer exact search preserves branches. The idea loses if adapters, retrieval, tools, or search contain the answer; if addressable memory is hidden in a growing cache; if recurrence fails beyond trained depth; if typed lanes interfere; or if a strong Transformer or hybrid wins on usefulness, safety, rights, latency, energy, and lifecycle cost. The current repository also has one non-Transformer count-table trace showing that the interface shell need not depend on attention. That is plumbing, not a competitive architecture result.

## 04:18–05:00 — Evidence boundary and handoff

**Visual description.** A ledger states `16 synthetic ABI cases`, `12 malicious
mutations rejected`, and `0 heterogeneous-kernel outcome runs`. The historical
KERC attempt is marked inadequate. A neutral end card points to Relational
Dimension Compilation and Polyadic Cognition.

**Narration.** The chapter remains Design rationale at argument support. A sixteen-case synthetic ABI checker rejects twelve malicious mutations, but the matched heterogeneous-kernel tournament produced zero outcome runs because the candidate and strongest-comparator envelope was unavailable. The historical KERC implementation was inadequate, leaving only two narrow finite observations with no semantic, multilingual, production, energy, or core claim. No architecture wins this video. The next chapter, Relational Dimension Compilation and Polyadic Cognition, takes one higher-order proposal through a stricter lifecycle while preserving lower-order rescue, reversible contraction, evidence boundaries, and the right to reject it.
