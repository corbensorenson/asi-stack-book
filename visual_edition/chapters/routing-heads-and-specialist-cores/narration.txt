This chapter asks a specific question: The architecture needs to allocate cognition across specialists rather than force one system to do every task. The tempting shortcut is insufficient: Monolithic scaling, static tool lists, and branded runtime references do not provide local memories, bounded authority, specialist lifecycle, readiness-aware routing, replay, or source-state separation.

The chapter's core claim is this: ASI scales through a lightweight routing head that selects bounded specialist cores with local tools, memory, and authority. Freeze the task, consumer, requested capability, acceptance predicate, authority and rights ceiling, risk, resource budget, deadline, and fallback obligation. Snapshot a versioned candidate registry containing specialist identity, implementation, capabilities, exclusions, local memory and tools, authority ceiling, readiness, evidence, cost, latency, load, dependencies, and lifecycle state. Compile semantic task or artifact identity separately from the physical model, expert, tool, node, human, or composite route.

A concrete implementation trace makes the proposal testable. Three public routing/runtime record schemas; a routing-decision lease harness with three valid and seven invalid fixtures; four valid and five invalid readiness/residual fixtures; a 300-example matched routing program over three seeds whose policies tied on a separable held-out split; a 60-request ambiguous single-model workload where the learned router selected 59 routes correctly but all 360 substantive candidates were wrong and its 20 correct outcomes were non-answer actions; two no-change or narrow evidence dispositions; and twenty Lean declarations classified as three direct predicate/projection checks and seventeen derived finite route or negative consequences.

The design can still fail. Semantic-fit laundering chooses a relevant specialist that lacks authority, readiness, useful competence, privacy, budget, or verification. Oracle laundering compares against a privileged or label-leaking route and calls the gap attainable routing quality. Route-label leakage lets training, features, prompts, thresholds, or evaluators observe held-out actions or outcomes. Router overconfidence suppresses clarification, fallback, abstention, or escalation on ambiguous or out-of-distribution work. The correct response is to stop, narrow, quarantine, compensate, or retain an owned residual rather than narrate uncertainty away.
