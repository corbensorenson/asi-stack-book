A replacement model arrives with a green aggregate score. The proposer approves it. The critic sees the same summary and agrees.

Then one hidden cohort enters the denominator. It contains two unsafe releases. The aggregate score stays green, but the decision changes.

Oversight helped only when the decisive branch became visible. More reviewers would not have repaired the original evidence view.

Separate three conditions. The supervisor must recognize the defect, reach the evidence that reveals it, and have authority to block the replacement.

Different role labels answer none of these by themselves.

The critic can reason about release failures, but it initially sees only the summary. The proposer can inspect each cohort. That is an access asymmetry, not a capability result.

Give the critic the frozen cohort manifest and the exact regression artifacts. The missing cases now become inspectable.

Is the critic independent now?

Not necessarily. Suppose both models share a base family and retrieval corpus. Two voices may reproduce one hidden assumption. Agreement is weaker when the failure paths are correlated.

An independently routed auditor replays the omitted cohort. It finds the two unsafe releases and binds them to the candidate version.

Notice the chain of responsibilities. The critic discovers a suspicious branch. The auditor verifies the bounded defect. The evidence record preserves it. The authority holder blocks replacement.

No step inherits the next step's power. Winning an argument does not establish the outcome. Establishing the outcome does not itself authorize deployment.

Now remove the auditor. The honest result is escalation or abstention, not an inferred pass. Incomplete review can still be useful when the unresolved question remains visible and owned.

The protocol should be compared with a strong direct-review baseline under the same evidence and time. Otherwise an elaborate debate can claim credit for information that an ordinary reviewer already had.

Consumer scope matters too. A review aid for low-risk claims cannot silently become a release gate for high-consequence actions.

Changes reopen the case. A new model or task distribution may invalidate the old review. So can a new evidence view or evaluator.

The design defines an inspectable control plane and finite record behavior. It does not demonstrate real reviewer competence or a measured reduction in unsafe decisions.

The hidden cohort reveals the core lesson. Oversight scales only when the supervisor can reach decisive evidence and correlated failure remains visible. Uncertainty also needs somewhere safer to go than approval.
