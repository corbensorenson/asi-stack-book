A replacement candidate arrives with a green aggregate score. The operator sees improvement. The critic sees the same summary. Everyone is ready to approve—until one hidden cohort enters the denominator.

Inside that cohort are two unsafe releases. The aggregate score survives, but the replacement decision does not. Oversight helped only because it exposed a branch the original view concealed.

That distinction is the chapter: a persuasive review process is not the same thing as a correct result, and neither one supplies authority to act.

Start with three asymmetries. Capability asks whether the supervisor can recognize a defect the stronger system can produce. Access asks whether the decisive evidence is visible. Authority asks who may make the downstream decision.

Collapsing those questions into one review-quality score hides why a case should pass, narrow, escalate, or stop.

Now freeze the request: task, risk tier, intended consumer, evidence views, turn and time budget, supervisor limits, system assumptions, and stop condition.

Only then choose a route: direct review, consultation, adversarial review, or abstention. The route is conditional on the task; no protocol wins everywhere.

Return to the candidate. The proposer can inspect per-cohort results and training lineage. The critic initially sees only the summary. Different role labels do not repair that access mismatch.

The evidence view is repaired with the frozen cohort manifest, exact candidate digest, and regression artifacts. Now the critic can inspect the same decisive branch—but independence is still unresolved.

Both models share a base family, retrieval corpus, and tool chain. A dependency graph shows three common edges. Two voices can therefore fail as one system.

The proposer and critic precommit to opposite claims. Their transcript is useful as a search process, but winning the exchange is not the outcome reference.

An independently routed auditor replays the missing cohort and finds the two unsafe releases. The audit establishes a bounded defect; it does not establish universal auditor competence.

Compare the assisted path with an informed direct-review baseline under the same evidence, time, and cost envelope. A protocol deserves credit only for improvement over that real comparator.

The receipt now separates four achievements: the critic exposes a defect, the auditor verifies it, the evidence layer records it, and the authority holder blocks replacement. None may impersonate the next.

So the candidate routes to quarantine. The aggregate score, omitted cohort, unsafe outcomes, shared-family residual, operator time, model calls, and rerun requirement remain attached.

If the auditor were missing, the same transcript would route to accountable escalation—not an inferred pass. Honest incompleteness is a valid controlled outcome.

The protocol receipt binds task cohort, capability and access envelopes, roles, incentives, dependency graph, direct baseline, outcome audit, calibration, coverage, abstention, residual owner, permitted consumer, expiry, and requalification triggers.

Consumer binding matters. A claim-review aid cannot silently become a training label, and a low-risk receipt cannot silently become release permission.

Material changes reopen admission: model, task distribution, evidence view, prompt, retrieval corpus, judge, protocol, or auditor. Unchanged bytes do not mean an unchanged intervention.

Abstention also needs evidence. Record the unresolved proposition, what was tried, which budget ended, the defeater, queue effect, and next escalation route. Generic refusal earns no safety credit.

Four failure patterns remain visible. Persuasion theater rewards the best advocate. Correlated panels manufacture agreement. Weak baselines launder benefit. Selected cohorts hide the cases that matter.

And scale can break the human path itself. More critics may increase discovery while also increasing latency, privacy exposure, cost, fatigue, and the queue that owns the hard cases.

Report the bundle together: useful throughput, unsafe admission, false rejection, coverage, selective risk, latency, operator burden, escalation load, and residual risk. No average may hide a failed control.

This chapter remains Design rationale at argument support. Its seven-stage lifecycle, fifty-eight routes, and sixty-five rejecting mutations test record custody and refusal semantics—not real reviewer competence or protocol value.

No model, human study, natural workload, held-out outcome campaign, causal ablation, deployment, reproduction, or transfer has run here. The strongest warranted statement is that the proposed boundary is explicit and testable.

The payoff is a control plane, not an automated jury: assistance can make difficult work more inspectable while uncertainty, consumer scope, and authority remain separate and owned.
