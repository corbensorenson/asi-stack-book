This chapter asks a specific question: After a command contract is accepted, the stack must choose which obligations become work, which alternatives remain candidates, what dependencies and observations make nodes feasible, how scarce capacity is scheduled, when dispatch is permitted, and how feedback changes only the affected future. A graph can be acyclic and still pursue the wrong decomposition, conceal uncertainty, request impossible context, select an inadequate route, violate authority or rights, erase counterfactuals, or create more coordination and recovery cost than useful work.

The chapter's core claim is this: Planning as a Control Layer should own a versioned, consumer-relative plan policy that selects and revises a partial order of obligations under uncertainty before execution. The policy binds the accepted command version; candidate decompositions and explicit abstention; typed nodes and dependency semantics; assumptions, observations, predictive-state and error models; context, tool, capability, authority, rights, resource, and verifier requirements; adequacy and utility predicates; lifecycle, dispatch, merge, stop, fallback, recovery, and replan rules; complete alternative and attempt denominators; and expected versus observed cost, latency, risk, and residuals.

A concrete implementation trace makes the proposal testable. The current minimum is a record-and-route scaffold: plan-graph, PlanForge-DAG, command, job, and semantic-atom schemas; three valid and ten invalid plan-execution fixtures; two valid scheduler-state traces and seven rejecting mutations; two valid runtime-replan traces and nine rejecting mutations; two accepted no-change transitions; and 33 theorem declarations grouped under seven manifest targets. These artifacts exercise exact record shapes, declared dependency order and cycles, selected lifecycle and dispatch routes, authority/stop preservation bits, local-repair scope, cost-ledger fields, digests, and no-promotion boundaries only. They contain no planner-generated natural plan, independent decomposition oracle, measured dependency truth, context-demand prediction, selected-tier adequacy, live scheduler, observed replan benefit, or support movement.

The design can still fail. Scope and obligation drift adds goals, means, parties, or success criteria that the accepted contract did not authorize. Decomposition theater produces plausible nodes that omit cross-cutting obligations, irreversible effects, verification, delivery, or recovery work. Dependency hallucination asserts causal or feasibility relations from textual order, model confidence, or graph convenience without evidence. Dependency omission allows a node to dispatch before required data, state, authority, rights, evidence, resource, or recovery prerequisites exist. The correct response is to stop, narrow, quarantine, compensate, or retain an owned residual rather than narrate uncertainty away.
