This chapter asks a specific question: A system can possess relevant context, tools, formal checks, citations, reviewers, and compute yet still lack enough independent, claim-specific verification work to justify the requested conclusion or support transition. Without an explicit adequacy boundary, context length, retrieval coverage, citation count, benchmark success, proof-checker output, model agreement, or review activity can be laundered into confidence while obligations, counterevidence, disagreements, costs, and unattempted checks disappear.

The design can still fail. Verification theater records planned checks, role labels, artifacts, or green status without observing competent claim-specific work and consequences. Context-length laundering treats more tokens, retrieved units, or nominal window size as joint verification capacity. Decomposition laundering reduces pairwise or higher-order work without naming boundary obligations, omitted interactions, completeness assumptions, or residuals.
