This chapter asks a specific question: The architecture needs a non-scale-only theory for how useful capability can increase without spending maximal cognition on every subtask. The tempting shortcut is insufficient: A scale-only allocation policy cannot, without additional state, select among routes using task-specific quality predicates, authority ceilings, total costs, residuals, or fallback readiness.

The chapter's core claim is this: On repeated workloads with multiple authorized routes, selecting the lowest-cost route that satisfies a fixed quality predicate and compiling reusable work can improve useful-task success per total contract cost over always-maximal and always-cheapest policies, provided authority, verification, residual, and fallback obligations remain intact. Select the lowest-cost authorized route in the declared candidate set that satisfies the fixed quality predicate; otherwise use the recorded fallback. Compile verified repeated work into versioned artifacts, tools, procedural memory, specialist routes, or regression tests, and charge their maintenance and revalidation costs.
