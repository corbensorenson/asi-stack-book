## 00:00–00:29 — Four prices, one fixed task

**Visual description.** Invoice 47 sits beside four horizontal routes priced at
one unit, four units, nine units, and human review. A fixed green test spans all
four lanes. Four probes travel through that same test. The invoice becomes a
typed task contract naming quality, authority, risk, deadline, and the cost
classes that must be counted.

**Narration.** Four routes can read the same invoice. One costs one unit, one
costs four, one costs nine, and one asks a human. Which route is efficient?
Price alone cannot answer. The route must preserve the invoice total, vendor,
date, and privacy rule under a fixed acceptance test. So begin with a task
ticket, not a model menu. The ticket names quality, authority, risk, deadline,
and the cost classes we promise to count.

## 00:29–01:12 — Why strongest and cheapest both fail

**Visual description.** The lanes acquire identities: versioned parser, small
specialist, frontier model, and human review, each with a magenta fallback.
Three ordinary invoices take the frontier route at nine units while the reuse
depot remains empty. The alternative “always cheapest” policy sends a damaged
invoice to the parser; its missing tax digit grows repair, review, and
correction weights. Gold authority gates and green quality gates then appear
before cost comparison.

**Narration.** Now the candidate routes become comparable: a versioned parser,
a small specialist, a frontier model, and human review. Each keeps its own
fallback. Always choosing the strongest route can preserve quality while
spending nine units on every ordinary invoice. Useful work survives, but reuse
never compounds. Always choosing the cheapest route looks better until a
damaged scan drops one tax digit. The one-unit answer creates repair, review,
and downstream correction. The Efficient ASI hypothesis asks for the
lowest-cost authorized route that can satisfy the fixed predicate, not the
lowest price on the menu.

## 01:12–01:53 — Acceptance, residuals, fallback, and the full bill

**Visual description.** A familiar invoice enters parser template version
three. Total, vendor, date, and privacy checks pass before acceptance. A damaged
scan replaces the first check with a red tax-field failure. A dashed line binds
the failure to an owned residual. The same ticket arcs to the specialist while
the failed parser attempt and its learning record remain visible. A copper
panel then expands over the route exchange and enumerates generation, context,

**Narration.** A familiar template may enter the versioned parser. Its output
earns acceptance only after the required checks pass and its assumptions remain
current. The damaged scan fails that check. The failure stays attached to the
task as a visible residual instead of becoming cheap success. Fallback then
escalates the same ticket to a stronger specialist or a human. The failed
attempt remains on the bill and in the learning record. Total contract cost
includes generation, context, routing, verification, repair, review, rollback
preparation, maintenance, delayed failure, and displaced work.

## 01:53–02:24 — Predict, pay, and compile reuse

**Visual description.** A balance compares the cheap route's one-unit attempt,
eight-unit repair, and three-unit review against a verified nine-unit route.
The left side resolves to twelve and drops below nine: visible cost is not total
cost. Four verified runs converge into parser version three inside a versioned
artifact depot. Assumptions, tests, and renewal obligations remain attached.

**Narration.** Now predict. If the cheap route costs one, repair costs eight,
and review costs three, is it more efficient than a verified nine-unit route?
No. Twelve is greater than nine, and the hidden-cost win disappears. Efficiency
belongs to the complete causal route, not its cheapest visible meter. Repeated
verified work can become a parser, cache, tool, memory, proof, or specialist.
But the artifact carries a version, assumptions, tests, and renewal cost.

## 02:24–03:01 — Expiry and regimes where selectivity loses

**Visual description.** Invoice template version four arrives. A red slash and
EXPIRED stamp cross parser version three; its renewal obligation turns red and
an arrow leads to revalidation. Six routed-resource dials appear for model,
context, search, precision, tools, and verification. A selector visits them
under one fixed contract. Novelty, uncertainty, and consequence gauges then
rise; their risk token moves toward the strongest route plus broad
verification, marked as a valid choice.

**Narration.** When the invoice format changes, the cached route expires.
Revalidation is not overhead added afterward; it prevents yesterday's saving
from becoming tomorrow's debt. The routed resource can be model capacity,
context, search, numerical precision, tools, or verification. Minimum viable
intelligence is a candidate policy, not an axiom. For novel, uncertain, or
high-consequence work, the strongest route plus broad verification may
dominate. The architecture must be allowed to learn that selectivity has no
value there.

## 03:01–03:38 — Forecasts, a fair test, and a joint score

**Visual description.** A cyan smooth-loss curve and a gold stepped operational
score share a panel; magenta forecast residuals remain plotted and a vertical
scanner crosses both charts. Four policies—always cheapest, always maximal,
adaptive, and monolith plus tools—move along matched lanes with no declared
winner. A final panel places accepted useful work over total contract cost and
adds unsafe effects, refusal, recovery, latency, human burden, tail failures,
and maintenance counters.

**Narration.** Scaling forecasts still matter, but a smooth loss curve is not a
capability guarantee. Preserve forecast residuals and watch operational
thresholds without declaring every jump real or illusory. A fair test freezes
natural tasks, then compares always-cheapest, always-maximal, adaptive routing,
and a strong monolith with tools under matched access and tuning. Score useful
accepted work against total contract cost. Count unsafe effects, false
refusals, recovery, latency, human burden, tail failures, and maintenance
together.
