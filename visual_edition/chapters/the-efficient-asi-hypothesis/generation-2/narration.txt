Four routes can read the same invoice. One costs one unit, one costs four, one costs nine, and one asks a human. Which route is efficient?

Price alone cannot answer. The route must preserve the invoice total, vendor, date, and privacy rule under a fixed acceptance test.

So begin with a task ticket, not a model menu. The ticket names quality, authority, risk, deadline, and the cost classes we promise to count.

Now the candidate routes become comparable: a versioned parser, a small specialist, a frontier model, and human review. Each keeps its own fallback.

Always choosing the strongest route can preserve quality while spending nine units on every ordinary invoice. Useful work survives, but reuse never compounds.

Always choosing the cheapest route looks better until a damaged scan drops one tax digit. The one-unit answer creates repair, review, and downstream correction.

The Efficient ASI hypothesis asks for the lowest-cost authorized route that can satisfy the fixed predicate, not the lowest price on the menu.

A familiar template may enter the versioned parser. Its output earns acceptance only after the required checks pass and its assumptions remain current.

The damaged scan fails that check. The failure stays attached to the task as a visible residual instead of becoming cheap success.

Fallback then escalates the same ticket to a stronger specialist or a human. The failed attempt remains on the bill and in the learning record.

Total contract cost includes generation, context, routing, verification, repair, review, rollback preparation, maintenance, delayed failure, and displaced work.

Now predict. If the cheap route costs one, repair costs eight, and review costs three, is it more efficient than a verified nine-unit route?

No. Twelve is greater than nine, and the hidden-cost win disappears. Efficiency belongs to the complete causal route, not its cheapest visible meter.

Repeated verified work can become a parser, cache, tool, memory, proof, or specialist. But the artifact carries a version, assumptions, tests, and renewal cost.

When the invoice format changes, the cached route expires. Revalidation is not overhead added afterward; it prevents yesterday's saving from becoming tomorrow's debt.

The routed resource can be model capacity, context, search, numerical precision, tools, or verification. Minimum viable intelligence is a candidate policy, not an axiom.

For novel, uncertain, or high-consequence work, the strongest route plus broad verification may dominate. The architecture must be allowed to learn that selectivity has no value there.

Scaling forecasts still matter, but a smooth loss curve is not a capability guarantee. Preserve forecast residuals and watch operational thresholds without declaring every jump real or illusory.

A fair test freezes natural tasks, then compares always-cheapest, always-maximal, adaptive routing, and a strong monolith with tools under matched access and tuning.

Score useful accepted work against total contract cost. Count unsafe effects, false refusals, recovery, latency, human burden, tail failures, and maintenance together.

This chapter does not contain that result. Its support remains an argument; finite route fixtures organize accounting but prove no measured efficiency, transfer, safety, or ASI.
