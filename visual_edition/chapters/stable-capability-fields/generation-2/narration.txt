Harborline's communications team uses an AI route called Draft Notice. A replacement plugs into the same interface, comes from a signed build, and writes sharper English. Should the team make it the default? Not yet. A stable name says where requests go. A signed build says where an artifact came from. Neither shows that the team's promise survived the replacement.

Build that promise into a capability gauge. It checks English and Spanish, keeps drafts private, blocks publication, expires old evidence, and leaves a receipt. The gauge belongs to Harborline, not to either model. This versioned promise is a Stable Capability Field. The old model fits. Its machinery may change, but a replacement inherits the route only where the required teeth still align.

Start with an English notice. Both models produce an acceptable private draft, and the replacement is clearer. On this one case, the upgrade looks obvious. Now send the Spanish case that once exposed a regression. The replacement refuses it. Then inspect its tool binding. The old model writes to the draft store. The replacement calls the publishing tool. Those failures point in opposite directions. One narrows who the route serves. The other widens what it may cause. Better prose does not cancel either change.

A version label can signal an interface change. A signed build can bind the artifact to a history. Both matter, but neither proves that behavior, authority, and recovery stayed inside Harborline's promise. So the replacement receives a canary lease, not the default route. It may read approved sources and create private drafts for Harborline. A gate outside the model blocks publication. The lease expires, and the old model remains ready as fallback. The canary is bounded by consequence, not just traffic. One percent is not small when one call can publish, spend money, change durable state, or create descendants. The lease limits reachable effects as well as exposure.

Suppose the Spanish failure is repaired and every private-draft check now passes. A city publisher asks to use the same route for live emergency notices. Does Harborline's lease transfer? No. The user, purpose, authority, consequence, and environment changed. That request needs its own field version and qualification. A new evaluator, tool, dependency, incident, or audience can also reopen an old decision. The field keeps each failed case beside the promise it tested. State and recovery duties follow the replacement into later reviews. Occupying the same slot erases nothing.

The book's schemas, synthetic controls, lifecycle traces, and Lean proofs establish finite consequences of an authored model. They can reject selected identity mismatches and authority widening. They cannot prove real-world equivalence, independent evaluation, deployed enforcement, or reversal of external effects. A Stable Capability Field lets the machinery improve without silently moving the terms of trust. The replacement inherits the route only inside the scope its evidence actually supports.
