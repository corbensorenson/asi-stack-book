Same service name. Same interface. Signed build. Sharper English. Harbor Line has every familiar reason to call the replacement an upgrade. But should it inherit the Draft Notice service? Hold the evidence against Harbor Line's promise. Not yet. A service name says where requests go. Provenance says where an artifact came from. Neither shows that Harbor Line's promise survived.

Build that promise into a capability gauge. It checks English and Spanish, keeps drafts private, blocks publication, expires old evidence, and leaves a receipt. The gauge belongs to Harbor Line, not to either model. This version-controlled promise is a Stable Capability Field. The old model fits. Its machinery may change, but a replacement inherits the service only where the required teeth still align.

Start with an English notice. Both models produce an acceptable private draft, and the replacement is clearer. On this one case, the upgrade looks obvious. Now send the Spanish case that once exposed a regression. The replacement refuses it. Then inspect its tool binding. The old model writes to the draft store. The replacement calls the publishing tool. Those failures point in opposite directions. One narrows who the service serves. The other widens what it may cause. Better writing does not cancel either change.

A version label can signal an interface change. A signed build can bind the artifact to a history. Both matter, but neither proves that behavior, authority, and recovery stayed inside Harbor Line's promise. So the replacement receives a canary lease, not the default service. It may read approved sources and create private drafts for Harbor Line. A gate outside the model blocks publication. The lease expires, and the old model remains ready as fallback. The canary is bounded by consequence, not just traffic. One percent is not small when one call can publish, spend money, change durable state, or create descendants. The lease limits reachable effects as well as exposure.

Suppose the Spanish failure is repaired and every private-draft check now passes. A city publisher asks to use the same service for live emergency notices. Does Harbor Line's lease transfer? Hold the lease against the changed use. No. The user, purpose, authority, consequence, and environment changed. That request needs its own field version and qualification. A new evaluator, tool, dependency, incident, or audience can also reopen an old decision. The field keeps each failed case beside the promise it tested. State and recovery duties follow the replacement into later reviews. Occupying the same slot erases nothing.

A finite model can prove that selected replacements preserve an authored identity, do not widen authority, and carry the events the contract requires. Synthetic controls can expose known mismatches and lifecycle failures. But none of that proves real-world equivalence, independent evaluation, deployed enforcement, or reversal of external effects. The gauge is a disciplined boundary, not a certificate of sameness. It lets the machinery improve without silently moving the terms of trust. A replacement inherits the service only inside the scope its evidence actually supports.
