Harborline has a route called "draft public notice." The team replaces its model and keeps the route name. Did the capability stay the same?

The new model writes better prose, but it refuses more Spanish requests and calls a different publishing tool. A stable label has concealed a changed contract.

Names are useful for routing. They are not evidence of substitutability.

Turn the route into a Stable Capability Field. The field describes what a consumer may expect, including valid inputs and observable outputs. It also defines how the route can fail or abstain.

Now bind one consumer: the Harborline communications workflow. The field promises a private draft, not publication. Its authority ceiling cannot rise when the implementation changes.

Qualification tests the old and new routes on the same versioned workload. The tests include the language case that exposed the regression.

Suppose both routes return the same English draft. Is that enough to replace the old implementation?

No. Matching one happy path says little about the failure behavior or the unmeasured consumers.

The candidate enters a bounded canary. It can read the approved source and produce a private artifact. It cannot call the publishing tool.

The Spanish regression appears. The field records which obligation failed and which consumers are affected. The candidate does not inherit the old route's qualification merely because it occupies the same slot.

Now imagine the new model improves after repair. Replacement still needs state and recovery planning. Cached decisions may depend on the prior implementation. External effects may need compensation rather than rollback.

The field therefore carries an expiry and invalidation triggers. A changed tool or evaluator can reopen qualification. So can a new audience.

Composition needs care too. Two individually qualified fields may fail when joined. The output of one can violate the precondition of the next.

The contract keeps that boundary visible. It names which composition was tested instead of granting a portable badge.

Finite schemas and transition models can reject mismatched identity or unauthorized widening. They cannot prove that two real systems are equivalent in deployment.

A stable route does not freeze one model forever. It preserves a consumer-visible promise while implementations change, and refuses replacement when the evidence no longer supports that promise.
