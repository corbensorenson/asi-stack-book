This chapter asks a specific question: A governed stack needs to replace models, prompts, tools, adapters, policies, data, evaluators, and runtime dependencies without letting a persistent capability name conceal changes in observable behavior, failure semantics, authority, evidence, affected consumers, or recovery obligations. Without a consumer-relative and time-bounded substitution contract, an apparent upgrade can silently redefine success, strand state, erase regressions, widen permissions, or inherit trust earned by a different artifact in a different environment.

The chapter's core claim is this: A Stable Capability Field should be a versioned, consumer-relative substitution contract rather than a capability name or implementation slot. It binds the field's semantic identity and observable interface—including admissible inputs, outputs, preconditions, postconditions, failures, abstentions, nondeterminism and resource bounds—to an authority ceiling, affected consumers, exact implementation and dependency identities, qualification context and lease, evaluator and evidence lineage, lifecycle and incident state, migration duties, preserved regressions, and effect-complete rollback obligations.

A concrete implementation trace makes the proposal testable. The current minimum is one public SCF schema with three valid and six expected-invalid records, one readiness/residual gate harness for synthetic route and rollback prerequisites, one deterministic lifecycle probe with two valid traces and six expected-invalid transition controls, and 18 theorem declarations in AsiStackProofs.StableCapabilityFields grouped under four manifest targets. These artifacts exercise record shape, scoped qualification fields, evaluator-separation declarations, authority ceilings, lifecycle routes, selected promotion and terminal-state predicates, regression and rollback prerequisites, fixture counts, and explicit no-promotion boundaries only.

The design can still fail. Identity laundering keeps the field name or compatible-looking version while observable behavior, failures, protected effects, or trust terms change. Interface theater treats matching signatures, schemas, or happy-path outputs as semantic substitutability. Qualification overreach generalizes one workload, consumer, evaluator, threat model, environment, or epoch to unrelated uses. Benchmark overfitting preserves the measured suite while degrading unmeasured obligations, rare failures, abstention quality, or downstream composition. The correct response is to stop, narrow, quarantine, compensate, or retain an owned residual rather than narrate uncertainty away.

A replacement that expands authority without a governance grant is rejected.
