## Art direction

The visual world is a dark claim laboratory built from cards, seals, facets,
rails, airlocks, shadows, and one-way projections. Cyan identifies claims and
observations, green marks satisfied bounded evidence, amber marks review and
transition authority, and magenta marks missing requirements, invalid
inferences, or adverse evidence. Labels, shapes, enclosure, position, and line
style duplicate every color meaning.

The claim atom is the persistent anchor. Evidence always approaches it through
a typed role; it never falls into a confidence cauldron. Downward and neutral
routes receive the same visual dignity as promotion. The adverse-evidence
shadow remains present through revision rather than appearing only as a warning
card.

## Evidence boundary

The synthetic eighteen-percent claim is a teaching example, not a result. The
visual explains the chapter's design rationale at argument support. It does not
promote any chapter claim, validate a deployed evidence lifecycle, establish
reviewer independence, demonstrate benchmark transfer, or establish safety,
state of the art, AGI, or ASI.
