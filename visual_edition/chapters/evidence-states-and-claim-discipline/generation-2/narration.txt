A result card says, "The memory adapter improved retrieval by eighteen percent." A green stamp lands on the card: proven.

Stop. What exactly did the evidence establish?

Freeze the sentence. Name the adapter and the workload. Add the baseline and the metric. The broad headline becomes a specific claim that can survive review without changing shape.

Now place three artifacts beside it. A paper explains why the adapter might work. A synthetic fixture shows that our code routes authored cases correctly. A held-out measurement would estimate the retrieval effect.

Those artifacts are not interchangeable. The paper supports a rationale. The fixture checks encoded behavior. Only the held-out run could directly test the eighteen-percent result.

Imagine seven quality indicators look excellent, but the evaluator is the same system that produced the answer. Should the strong indicators average away the missing independence?

No. Evidence quality is a vector, not a single score. A failure on a load-bearing dimension can stop the transition even when other dimensions are strong.

Move the claim toward an evidence gate. The gate is specific to this sentence. It asks for the artifact and method required by the stronger wording.

The nearby citation does not open it. The source may never have tested this implementation. The fixture does not open it either. Correct behavior on authored examples is not a measured effect on held-out data.

So the claim stays where it is. The ledger records the missing run rather than letting polished prose imply that it happened.

Now suppose the held-out experiment runs, but its positive control fails. The instrument could not detect a known difference. That result does not prove no improvement. It weakens the test itself.

Evidence can move a claim in more than one direction. It can strengthen the wording or narrow its scope. It can also leave the claim unchanged, weaken it, or refute it.

Negative results stay attached to the lineage. A later revision may answer them, but it may not erase the failed control or contradiction that shaped the new claim.

Nor does one accepted result move every neighboring claim. A retrieval gain would not establish deployment safety. A formal proof would not establish that a runtime enforces its assumptions.

The local schemas and finite checks show how records and transitions can be constrained. They do not validate the method in deployment or supply the missing retrieval measurement.

The payoff is disciplined revision. The book can become clearer whenever prose improves. It becomes better supported only when evidence that matches a particular claim passes that claim's particular gate.
