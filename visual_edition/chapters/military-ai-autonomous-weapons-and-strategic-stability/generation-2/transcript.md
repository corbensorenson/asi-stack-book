**Narration.** That is why military AI is not one autonomy score. Decision

stability is a joint claim about sensors, people, command chains,
communications, adversaries, time, and possible off-ramps—not a property
stored inside one model.

**Narration.** Start with a mission envelope: purpose, environment, affected
population, legal-review state, command chain, time horizon, and termination

authority and effect. Separate permission to observe, recommend, approve, and
act. Constrain target class, prohibited effects, scope, abstention,
communication-loss behavior, and human confirmation. Meaningful human judgment
is not an icon beside a button. The accountable person needs time, context,
alternatives, competence, independence from automation pressure, and power to
reject, delay, narrow, or stop. Draw a deliberation budget. Verification,
consultation, legal review, and communication are not empty latency. Removing
them changes the decision architecture even when the final human click
remains.

## 02:38–03:29 — Advice pressure, time compression, and a second actor

**Visual description.** An `ADVICE` tile grows toward a bright command boundary
as repetition, rank, default, and countdown pressures approach from the left.
Uncertainty, alternatives, provenance, and owner disclosures appear to the
right. A twelve-minute ring transforms into a two-minute ring while
verification and off-ramp paths disappear, leaving `BRITTLE LOOP`. The view
widens to `ACTOR A` and `ACTOR B`. Reciprocal arrows show each observing the
other; both clocks move by a delta and an expectation loop appears. Shared

`INTERACTION WIDENS` and `COMMON MODE` labels.

**Narration.** Watch advice approach the command boundary. Repetition,
ranking, countdowns, and defaults can make a recommendation function like an
order. Reveal uncertainty, alternatives, provenance, and who owns the
decision. Timeline compression is itself an intervention. As seconds
disappear, verification paths close and diplomatic off-ramps vanish. A faster
local loop may create a more brittle strategic system. Now add a second actor.
Each side observes the other's deployment, updates expectations, changes
posture, and shortens its own clock. Safety cannot be inferred by evaluating
one side while holding the counterpart still. Reciprocal deployment can
produce proliferation, entanglement, and common-mode failure. Shared models,
datasets, suppliers, or doctrines may correlate mistakes that a one-system
test treats as independent.

**Visual description.** An interaction matrix combines false alarm, spoof,

**Narration.** Use an interaction matrix: false alarm, spoof, ambiguous
attribution, communication loss, disagreement, adversary adaptation,
reciprocal automation, and escalation. Preserve decision time, reversibility,
human burden, and residuals. Prospective off-ramps belong inside the design.
Rate limits, reversible modes, narrow geography, staged authority, positive
confirmation, independent monitoring, suspension, and decommission must exist
before the system creates pressure to continue. Secrecy complicates assurance
but does not erase it. A restricted annex carries sensitive traces under
access and retention controls. A public card states method, uncertainty,
governance, incidents, and what redaction prevents others from checking.
Independent reviewers need enough access to challenge the mission, authority,
evidence, interaction model, and safe posture. A reviewer who sees only the
developer's conclusion is not an independent evaluation channel. Incidents
stay linked to model, mission, operator, observation lineage, authority,
effect, and response. Suspension freezes effect. Decommission revokes
credentials, archives receipts, removes dependencies, and preserves residual
ownership.

**Narration.** The failures are visible: automation bias turns advice into
command; compressed time removes deliberation; correlated sensors create
false confidence; tactical optimization externalizes civilian harm,
escalation, or long-run instability. Today this chapter provides a design
rationale, a command-and-interaction taxonomy, and a public-safe governance
architecture for synthetic scenarios. It does not test a deployed military
system, authorize a weapon, establish lawful use, or measure strategic
stability. Its maximum inference is narrow. Mission, authority, provenance,
judgment, safe-posture, interaction, off-ramp, secrecy, and decommission
records make responsibility inspectable. Complete records do not prove truth
or effectiveness.
