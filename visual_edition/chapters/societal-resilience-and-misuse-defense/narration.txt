This chapter asks a specific question: Model safeguards and internal incident command cannot by themselves protect people, institutions, and critical services when prevention fails, misuse diffuses across platforms, or harms exploit social and infrastructural vulnerabilities. The tempting shortcut is insufficient: A classifier, terms of service, model refusal, takedown queue, or company-local incident process sees only one control point. Fraud, sexual abuse, cyber campaigns, synthetic media, manipulation, and vulnerable-user harms cross organizations, jurisdictions, languages, identities, and recovery systems.

The chapter's core claim is this: Societal misuse defense should be operated as a domain-specific resist-absorb-recover-adapt network with shared incident identity, lawful minimal telemetry, harmed-party routes, cross-organization escalation, defensive service levels, evidence-preserving response, correction, and residual ownership; prevention metrics alone establish neither resilience nor acceptable harm. Maintain domain playbooks for fraud and impersonation, child and image-based abuse, cyber misuse, biological/chemical misuse, manipulation, and vulnerable-user harms. Define shared incident identifiers, severity, evidence minimums, privacy boundaries, escalation contacts, and status transitions across participating organizations. Layer prevention, friction, detection, rapid patching, victim support, correction, recovery, and adaptation instead of depending on one classifier.
